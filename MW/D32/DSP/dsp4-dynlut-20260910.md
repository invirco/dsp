# S15 — the dynamics integration: the clock settled, the table's home measured, and the level→gain table in the generator

Session S15, 2026-09-09/10. Bench: rev C unit, shipping CPLD
`a1f6672af6c3`, `dsp4-pcm-slave`. Contract `defs-v2026.09.08.4`,
unchanged. `shipping.config` unchanged.

**Everything in this session is behind a switch that is off by default,
and that is proved rather than asserted: built at the shipping defaults
the tree produces `6ebd0807` / `a3582da1`, byte for byte what the pre-S15
tree produces from the same command.**

---

## 0. CCLK, settled — and S14-7 was looking at an unconfigured part

S14 left the record with a decode and no measurement, and said so:
`capacity.sh` decoded the running CGU as 983.04 MHz where the P2.2 record
(2026-08-21) had measured 491.52 off the core timer. A decode says what
the CGU was *asked* for. It cannot say what the part is *doing*. So the
part was asked, twice, by two methods that share nothing.

### (a) The CGU registers, decoded by hand

Read back over the diag peek window on a booted and configured pair:

```
CGU0_CTL  0x00005000     CGU0_DIV  0x451442C1     CGU0_STAT 0x00000005
```

From the HRM's own formula, with SYS_CLKIN0 = 24.576 MHz:

```
MSEL = (CTL >> 8) & 0x7F = 80      DF = CTL & 1 = 0      CSEL = DIV & 0x1F = 1
fPLLCLK = CLKIN * MSEL / (2 * (DF+1)) = 983.040 MHz
fCCLK   = fPLLCLK / CSEL             = 983.040 MHz
```

The decode is checked, not trusted: the same three lines reproduce every
row of `cgu_init.asm`'s own table — `0x2800` → 491.520, `0x4000` →
786.432, `0x5000` → 983.040 — which is what fixes MSEL at bits 14:8 and
puts the PLL's fixed /2 *inside* fPLLCLK rather than after it. (The
`0x40000000` in `CGU0_DIV` is the UPDT bit latched by the write sequence;
the divisor field is `0x051442C1`, exactly as `cgu_init.asm` records.)

### (b) The core timer, against two wall clocks that are not the DSP's

The core timer decrements TCOUNT once per CCLK cycle *by construction*
and reloads from TPERIOD, so `DIAG_TICKS` advances at exactly
CCLK / DIAG_TPERIOD Hz and nothing in that chain reads the CGU.
DIAG_TPERIOD is 983,040 in this build, which `DIAG_BUILD_CFG` states in
its own bits 18:17.

| clock it is measured against | chip 1 | chip 2 |
|---|--:|--:|
| the Pi's crystal, 20–120 s dwell, reads bracketed and mid-pointed | 983.03 MHz | 983.01 MHz |
| the CPLD's 48 kHz audio transport (ticks per block × 3000 blocks/s) | 983.04 MHz | 983.02 MHz |

and on the capacity arm itself, where the instrument now takes the
measurement as a matter of course:

```
chip 1  CCLK decoded 983040000  MEASURED 983037159  (tick  999.997 /s)
chip 2  CCLK decoded 983040000  MEASURED 983073610  (tick 1000.034 /s)
```

Three parts per million on chip 1, thirty-four on chip 2.

### The verdict, and what it does to the record

**CCLK is 983 MHz. The budget is 327,680 cycles per block,
`capacity.sh`'s decode is right, and the D32 fit survives it — no
percentage in the record moves.**

### S15-1 — and the 491.52 MHz sighting has a mechanism

At the start of this session the pair on the part read **CGU0_CTL
0x00002800, the CGU reset row, and a diag tick of 499.995/s — 491.52 MHz,
exactly half** — with `DIAG_BUILD_CFG 0xCF45FF10`, i.e. an image *built*
with `DSP4_CCLK_TARGET=983`. `DIAG_BLK_OVERRUN` was tracking
`DIAG_FRAME_COUNT` one for one: the part was missing essentially every
block.

The cause is in the same register block that reported it. **`BOOT_STAGE`
read 5 — `DIAG_STAGE_WAITCFG`, "interrupts on, waiting for host
config".** `_cgu_raise_cclk` is called from `_product_config_commit`,
deliberately (D10's objection was to relocking the PLL with the boot
kernel's SPI transfer still in flight). A part that was never configured
therefore runs on the CGU reset divisors — at half the clock its own
budget assumes, with a graph that cannot fit in half a budget.

It is not the firmware and it is not the app: booting the same staged
`blk_*` images (`ac65ad38` / `e5dce9e4`) through `dsp4_boot.py` +
`dsp4_config.py` brought both chips to `0x00005000` and 983.04 MHz, and
so did `systemctl restart matrix-app` — chip 1 then read 0.00 % overruns
over 60,014 blocks, chip 2 2.49 %, which is a separate question and is
recorded here rather than chased. **The bench had been left booted but
unconfigured, and "booted" is not "running".** S14's bench-restored note
is true as written and does not distinguish the two.

**The fix is in the instrument, permanently.** `tools/pi/dsp4_capacity.py`
now measures CCLK on every capacity row — DIAG_TICKS across its own
dwell, with TPERIOD taken from `DIAG_BUILD_CFG`'s own bits — reports it
beside the decode, **derives the budget from the measured clock**, and
prints an explicit disagreement line if the two differ by more than 2 %.
A capacity figure can no longer be quoted against a clock nobody
measured.

## 1. The table's home: DM, and S14's premise was wrong

S14 measured a DM-resident table and named the risk itself: 96 dynamics
nodes × their own table, "which would have to go to L2", where an L2
per-sample gather is a different instrument and unmeasured. Both halves
of that were tested.

### The linker map says DM

| | chip 1 | chip 2 |
|---|--:|--:|
| DM data + stack, free at the shipping configuration | 123,852 bytes | 145,788 bytes |
| the tables, 337 words × 32 / × 28 nodes | 43,136 bytes | 37,744 bytes |
| DM after, measured on the built arm | 79.6 %, 76,438 free | 73.0 %, 101,396 free |

**It fits, with room.** The 1,024-word overflow `dyn_tables_fx.asm`
records was `sec_stak`, a different pool from `sec_dmda` and its overflow
tier; reading it as a DM-wide limit is what put L2 into the design.

### And the rig measured what L2 would have cost

Four rungs added to `dyn_shootout.asm` — same harness, same envelope,
same gain application, so what differs is the thing named. Cycles per
sample for a **pair** of channels; the per-channel column is half.

| rung | c/s-pair | c/s/chan |
|---|--:|--:|
| the gain computer TODAY, 6-term polynomials | 215.2 | 107.6 |
| **the level→gain LUT, table in DM** | **54.1** | **27.0** |
| the same, table in L2 | 76.1 | 38.1 |
| the whole COMPRESSOR body, today | 284.3 | 142.2 |
| **the same, LUT with a DM table** | **123.1** | **61.6** |
| the same, LUT with an L2 table | 146.2 | 73.1 |
| 4 words copied L2 → DM per sample slot | 46.0 | — |
| the whole GATE body, today | 142.1 | 71.1 |
| **the same, LINEAR-domain threshold** | **65.0** | **32.5** |

* **L2 costs +22.1 cycles per sample-pair on the gain computer (+40.9 %)
  and +23.0 on the whole compressor body (+18.7 %).** Not prohibitive —
  the LUT out of L2 still takes the compressor from 142.2 to 73.1 per
  channel — but not free either.
* **Paging a table from L2 into a DM slot is worse than gathering from L2
  directly, for any table over 32 words.** The copy is 11.5 cycles a
  word, so a 337-word page is 3,876 cycles a block against the 354 the
  direct L2 gather costs over the same block. The break-even is
  22.1 × 16 / 11.5 = 32 words and the smallest table that clears the
  accuracy bar is ten times that.

**So: DM, and the number that goes into the generator is 54.1 / 123.1.**

## 2. S15-4 — the S14 rig's interpolation fraction was sign-extended, and a cycle rig cannot see that

`tools/dsp/dyn_lut_design.py` was written to answer the error question
the rig cannot: it computes the index **bit for bit the way the kernel
does** and sweeps the resulting gain against the repo's own fixed-point
reference. The first thing it found was in the kernel.

`LUT_INDEX_SIMD` took the interpolation fraction as

```
r4 = lshift r3 by K          /* r3 = mantissa fraction, Q0.31 */
```

Shifting a Q0.31 value left by K to discard the K bits already spent on
the sub-index pushes what is left **into bit 31**, and the interpolation
multiply is `mrf = r5 * r4 (ssi)` — *signed*. A fraction of 0.75 arrives
as −0.25 and the interpolation runs backwards out of its cell.

Measured, at K = 4 on the shipped compressor defaults:

| | worst error, 0 to −100 dBFS |
|---|--:|
| the fraction as S14's rig computed it | **0.389 dB** |
| with the mask (`r4 = r4 and 0x7FFFFFFF`) | **0.005 dB** |

The tell is the shape rather than the size: the error halved with each
doubling of the point count, which is *first order*, and linear
interpolation of a smooth function is second order. **An error that
improves at the wrong rate is an arithmetic fault, not a mesh that is too
coarse.**

**It could not have shown in S14.** The rig's own header says why: *the
table's contents do not change the instruction stream*, so a cycle
measurement is blind to what the table returns. S14 measured the right
cycles for the wrong arithmetic, and modelled the error separately
against what the comment said the kernel did.

**The cost of being right is four instructions** — the mask, and the high
clamp a table that no longer spans all 32 octaves needs — and that is the
whole of the difference between S14's 49.1 and this session's 54.1 cycles
per sample-pair.

## 3. The point count, measured

Index = `leftz` exponent + top K mantissa bits, so the table is
log-spaced by construction; `OCT_LO = 10` drops the octaves below
−100 dBFS, where every one of these curves is exactly flat, so the clamp
there is correct rather than approximate.

Worst error over 0 to −100 dBFS against `fixed_ref.comp_gain`, at the
shipped defaults (COMP −20 dB / 4:1 / 6 dB knee; LIMITER −0.5 dB):

| K | pts/octave | words | COMP | LIMITER |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 2 | 4 | 85 | 0.045 dB | 0.247 dB |
| 3 | 8 | 169 | 0.011 dB | 0.028 dB |
| **4** | **16** | **337** | **0.004 dB** | **0.025 dB** |
| 5 | 32 | 673 | 0.001 dB | 0.018 dB |

The defaults are not the bar. Over the **full documented parameter
sweep** — 81 sets, thresholds to −60 dB, ratios to 100:1, knees 0/6/18 dB
— K = 3 **fails** at 0.135 dB (the limiter at a −3 dB threshold, whose
infinite ratio and hard knee is the sharpest corner any of these curves
has) and **K = 4 passes at 0.0950 dB** against PW's 0.1 dB ruling.

**K = 4, 337 words a node, is the shipped choice, and the margin is
5 %.** K = 5 halves the error again for 673 words and still fits DM; it
is the lever if 5 % is judged too thin. A design pass that nudges the
knots bounding a kinked cell, rather than pinning them to the curve, is
worth about 2× and is in the tool.

**The GATE is confirmed untabelable, independently of S14-3.** Its static
curve is a step, and the same sweep reads **41–65 dB** of error at every
mesh from 4 to 64 points per octave. No mesh fixes a discontinuity.

## 4. The two-table blend does not work, and what replaces it

S14 proposed blending two designed tables while a parameter ramps,
because the design step is ~211 instructions per point and cannot run per
block. The rig priced the blend at +49.1 cycles per sample-pair — an
affordable-looking number. `dyn_lut_design.py --blend` priced its
**error**:

| threshold ramp | one designed table | blending two |
|---|--:|--:|
| −20 → −10 dB | 0.011 dB | **1.13 dB** |
| −40 → −20 dB | 0.011 dB | **3.12 dB** |
| −10 → −3 dB | 0.011 dB | **0.66 dB** |
| −60 → −3 dB | 0.011 dB | **20.20 dB** |

Against a 0.1 dB bar. The reason is structural and is the same one that
kills the gate: **two gain curves whose knees sit at different levels do
not interpolate into the curve whose knee is between them.** The blend is
exact at both ends and wrong in the middle, which is what every
plausible-looking interpolation of a family of kinked curves does.

**What ships instead costs nothing and is exact.** A node whose converted
parameters have moved restarts its design and **runs the polynomial gain
computer — the path that ships today — until the table is finished.** The
table is the steady-state path; the ramp path is unchanged and exactly
correct; there is no second table. The changeover happens at a block
boundary between two curves that agree to the table's own error
(0.004 dB at the defaults), so nothing steps.

`DYN_LUT_CHUNK` is 16 points a block: ~3,400 cycles, 1.0 % of a block for
a node that is rebuilding, and a whole table takes 22 blocks — 7.3 ms.

## 5. What is in the generator

Behind **`DSP4_DYN_LUT`** (default 0):

* per COMPRESSOR node, `_comp_lut_<nid>[337]` in DM, a design cursor, and
  the four converted parameters the table was designed for — the key is
  the *converted* words, because two float thresholds that quantise to
  the same Q6.25 word describe the same curve and must not trigger a
  rebuild;
* `_dyn_lut_step` — the block-rate design step, **one shared routine**
  rather than forty instructions inlined in two bodies of each of
  thirty-two nodes (the first build that had it inline linked chip 1 with
  **66 bytes** of code pool free);
* `_dyn_lut_fill` — the design itself, calling the existing
  `_compgain_fx`, so the table is baked from the arithmetic that ships;
* `_dyn_lut_gain` — the scalar lookup — and `LUTGAIN_SIMD`, the paired
  one, both in `src/lib/dyn_lut.h` so the design step, the two lookups
  and the host-side design tool cannot disagree about the grid;
* `_comp_pair_blk` gains a **second sample loop**, chosen once per block
  on `_dlut_live`, which the pair driver sets only when **both** channels
  have a finished table. One channel on the table and the other on the
  polynomial is not expressible in a paired kernel and would not be
  wanted if it were: it would put the table's error on a channel
  boundary instead of on a block boundary.

Behind **`DSP4_GATE_LINTHR`** (default 0), which now builds beside
`DSP4_SIMD_DYN` where it used to `#error`:

* `_gate_thrq_<nid>` holds **2^thr in Q4.28 — linear — in every path**.
  The per-sample body, the block kernel and the pair kernel all convert
  it from the float parameter once per block and all compare the envelope
  against it directly, so there is no path in which one of them reads a
  log value out of that word. That is what makes the two arithmetics safe
  to have in one tree, and it is why the `#error` could be lifted rather
  than worked around.
* The paired GATE loses the whole of `LOG2Q_SIMD` and its log2(0) guard:
  **142.1 → 65.0 cycles per sample-pair, 71.1 → 32.5 per sample per
  channel, 54.2 %.**

The objection the `#error` recorded was real: the two forms *are*
different arithmetic. The difference is a threshold shift of at most
0.0002 dB — both directions go through polynomials whose worst error over
0 to −100 dBFS is 0.0001 dB — which was worth refusing against a
0.0001 dB spec and is 1/500th of PW's 0.1 dB ruling of 2026-09-09.

**And `DIAG_BUILD_CFG2` now carries four switches it could not see**
(S12-7, closed): `DSP4_C2_BQ_GRAPH` — the one that made two S12 candidate
images read back the same word and differ in *audio* — plus
`DSP4_BQ_SIMD_PIPE`, `DSP4_DYN_LUT` and `DSP4_GATE_LINTHR`. At the
shipping defaults all four are zero and the word is unchanged.

LUT cannot ship on chip 1 until the code pool has relief, and it says
where to look — the per-node inlining that S13 and S14 bought cycles with

| `dyn_lut_design.py --selftest` (new) | **PASS** |
| `dyn_state_bound.py` §5 (new, the table form) | **PASS** |
| `dyn_state_bound.py` overall | FAIL — **pre-existing and unrelated**, see below |

`dyn_state_bound.py` returns FAIL on §3, the gate and talkback sidechain
biquads, where H = 5 is reachable and nothing sizes the header word. That
verdict is **unchanged from HEAD** — the same script at the pre-S15 commit
returns the same FAIL — and it is about a sidechain filter, not about the
dynamics path this session touched.

`dyn_lut_design.py --selftest` is the regression guard for the S15-4
class specifically, and it is three properties rather than a smoke test:
a grid point must round-trip to its own index with a **zero** fraction;
the fraction must satisfy `0 <= frac < 2^31` for every sample of every
cell — **which is exactly the property S15-4 violated**; and a sampled
table must return the sampled value at every knot and stay inside the
cell between two of them.

### The table form's own state bound, added to `dyn_state_bound.py` as §5

Every word in the table is the output of `_compgain_fx`, whose range is
`[0, 1<<28]` — checked, not asserted, over the parameter corners and the
whole index range. The per-sample interpolation

```
g = T[i] + rns((T[i+1] - T[i]) * frac, 31),    0 <= frac < 1
```

is therefore a **convex combination of two values in that interval** and
lies in it: it cannot wrap, for the same kind of reason the envelope
one-pole cannot — not because the inputs are gentle, but because the
operator is an average. The intermediate is under 2^59 in an 80-bit MR.

**So the table form deletes a saturate from the per-sample path and adds
none.** `_exp2q_fx`'s saturate still runs — once per table point in the
DESIGN step, not once per sample in the audio path. That is the
"saturation once" the question asks about, and under the table it is once
per *design* rather than once per *sample*.

## 9. What is NOT done, and it is named rather than implied

* **The LIMITER is not on the table.** `_lim_pair_blk` (`src/lib2`,
  chip 2 only) and the LIMITER node template still run the polynomial.
  S14-4 measured that 162 of its 251 instructions per sample-pair are
  log2 + exp2, so it is the single largest remaining prize, and **chip 2
  has the room for it** — 54.6 % of its code pool and 73.0 % of its DM —
  which is exactly why it is the next thing rather than a hard one. The
  form is settled and the shared parts (`dyn_lut.h`, `_dyn_lut_step`,
  `_dyn_lut_fill`, `_dyn_lut_gain`) are already in the tree; what is
  missing is the node template's tables and the kernel's second sample
  loop, the same two edits the COMPRESSOR took.
* **The audio-domain verdict on `DSP4_DYN_LUT` was not obtained.**
  `dsp4_comp_gr.py` was run against the LUT arm and **returned its own
  refusal** — *"NOT COMPRESSING (< 1 dB of reduction) — this capture
  proves nothing about the gain computer"* — with a settled value of
  **0**, i.e. the capture path returned nothing rather than the
  compressor failing to compress. A capacity arm has
  `DSP4_PROFILE_SIGNAL=0` and `matrix-app` stopped, so there is no
  signal for an envelope to follow; the tool is working exactly as
  designed in refusing a verdict it cannot support (its own rule: *a
  comparison that cannot fail is not evidence*). Getting that verdict
  needs an arm with a signal source, which is a build, and it was not
  taken.
* **`famverify` was not run against the `DSP4_DYN_LUT` arm either**, and
  `famverify.sh`'s own header says why it is awkward: its `_scope_tap`
  witness (`DSP4_SCOPE_BLK_TAP=1`) does **not** fit alongside
  `STRIP_FUSED` + `SIMD_DYN` on chip 1 at all, and the LUT leaves 228–292
  bytes of code pool. The walk would have to run with the tap off.

  **What IS proved on the part, and it is not nothing:** the design step
  produces a table **bit-identical to the model, 337 of 337 words** (§10);
  the pair kernel demonstrably takes the LUT loop, because the capacity
  delta is 89 % of the rig's per-sample figure and would be zero if it did
  not (§7); the arm boots, configures and runs **270,082 blocks per chip
  with zero overruns**; and the switch is byte-for-byte inert at its
  default. **What is not proved is the last link — that the gain the LUT
  produces reaches the audio unchanged — and that is the first thing the
  next session should close.**
* **The staged `dyn_*` / `flr_*` pairs are built at
  `DSP4_C2_BQ_GRAPH=0`** — the audio-correct setting per S12-5 — while
  the capacity arms above were taken at `=1` so they are directly
  comparable with S14's row. Chip 2's capacity for the staged
  configuration is therefore not this session's measurement.

## 10. The table read off the part, and what that instrument found

`tools/pi/dsp4_dyn_lut_check.py` reads a node's designed table out of DM
over the diag peek window and **diffs it word for word** against
`dyn_lut_design.py`'s model — built from the node's OWN key, read off the
part, so the only question left is whether the design step and the model
agree about the grid and the curve, not about parameter rounding.

On `C1_COMP_01`, cursor 337 of 337, key `0xF95B2C40 0x60000000
0x00000000 0x00000000`:

```
DYN_LUT TABLE MATCH: 337 of 337 words identical to the host model,
design step and grid agree exactly.
```

**It got there in two runs, and the first one is the point of the
instrument.** That run read **336 of 337 identical and one mismatch, at
index 336** — the table's last word, the guard the interpolation reads as
`T[i+1]` for the last real cell. Its grid point is octave 31, whose
envelope is `1 << 31`: **outside a signed 32-bit word.** On the part it
arrives negative, `_compgain_fx` takes its `x_abs <= 0` path and returns
**unity**, so the top cell would have interpolated from a heavily reduced
gain back UP to unity between +17.8 and +18.1 dBFS. The host model, in
Python's unbounded integers, computed the honest gain for 8.0 and
disagreed. Both are now clamped to Q4.28's largest positive value.

The defect is small — it is above full scale and would need an envelope
18 dB over 0 dBFS to be reached. **What matters is that nothing else in
the tree could have found it.** The cycle rig is blind to table contents
by construction (§2); the error sweep runs over 0 to −100 dBFS and never
looks above full scale; `famverify` asks whether a family is live, not
what its curve does at +18 dBFS. A word-for-word diff between the part
and the model is a fourth instrument, and this is what it is for.

## 11. The design step's peak, which a capacity average cannot see

Every node's key starts unmatched, so at the **first `CONFIG_COMMIT` all
thirty-two of chip 1's compressors restart their design in the same
block.** At `DYN_LUT_CHUNK = 16` that is 32 × 16 × ~211 ≈ 108,000 cycles
on top of the graph — a third of a block — for the 22 blocks it takes to
fill.

`capacity.sh` cannot see it: its overrun figure is a **delta** across a
dwell that begins *after* the boot and config ladder, and the burst is
inside that ladder. `_proc_cyc_max` can, because nothing resets it:

| `DYN_LUT_CHUNK` | chip 1 avg | chip 1 `_proc_cyc_max` | blocks to fill |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| 16 | 60.27 / 60.45 % | **124.06 %** — over budget | 22 (7.3 ms) |
| **4** | 60.26 / 60.49 % | **96.93 / 97.04 %** | 85 (28 ms) |

**The average is unchanged and the worst block drops below budget.**
`DYN_LUT_CHUNK = 4` is the shipped value. 28 ms of the exact polynomial
after a parameter move is inaudible, and it is the arithmetic that ships
today.

This is the one place in the design where `_proc_cyc_max` — which S13-2
correctly distrusts because it latches configuration transients — is
measuring exactly the transient it is being asked about.

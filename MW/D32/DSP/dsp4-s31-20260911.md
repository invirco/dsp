# S31 — the lost MCU announce on the rev C unit

Session 31, 2026-09-11. Bench: rev C unit, `app@192.168.1.219` (MW-D24-2).
App under test: `/home/app/app`, md5 `774752174a7f59637a082031f3fd4231`,
the Aug 18 build — **unchanged for the whole session**. No `appUpdate` was
ever staged, the md5 was re-read at every one of the eight restarts, and
every restart logged **0 `AnalogBringUp` lines**. The analog bring-up build
was not deployed during this session, so the ladder below is the OLD app
throughout and there is no second ladder to report.

The DSP was not touched: no SHARC reflash, no CPLD flash, no `~/dspboot`
staged pair read or written, no overlay change, `AN_EN` never asserted, the
74HC595 chain and the +48 V never approached. The only hardware this session
drove is the MX bus UART, with the two characters the app itself writes.

---

## 1. The headline

**The announce is lost ON THE WIRE, and the loss is a COLLISION between
H1S1's and H1S4's startup announcements on the shared multi-drop MX bus.**
The three slaves answer `S_RUN` 208 / 211 / 215 ms after it — H1S1 and H1S4
**3 ms apart**, against ~1.2 ms for one line at 115200 — and 6 of 40 trials
(**15 %**) garble the pair into one corrupt line or wedge the bus outright.
It reproduces with `matrix-app` **stopped and no app in the path at all**, at
the same rate the app ladder shows (1 in 8), so it is MCU firmware and bus
arbitration, not the app's serial reader.

**S27-6 was right about the pair and wrong about the immunity.** "H1S1 and
H1S4 fail together or not at all" is confirmed and now explained — they
collide with each other, and 5 of the 6 failures garble exactly that pair and
leave H1S3 clean. "H1S3 announces every time and is never the one that fails"
is wrong: this session's one failing restart lost H1S3 and H1S4 and kept
H1S1, which is the second grade of the same fault.

**S29's "0 of 3 five times" names a real state** — the wire reproduces a
grade where the bus goes silent after `// debug only` and even the heartbeat
stops — **but the evidence S29 quoted for it never supported it**:
`journalctl -u matrix-app` has never carried MCU text on this unit, on any
restart, including the seven this session that verified 3 of 3.

The app has a separate, real defect: it starts its only serial reader *after*
`S_RUN`, and reads the bus 5× slower than the bus delivers it (§4). That is
why the fault has read as an app race for four sessions, but it is not the
cause.

### 2.1 The window is not the race

From the clean restart 8, the app's own timeline:

| time | event | Δ |
|---|---|---|
| 08:11:31.3288 | open `/dev/serial0` at 115200 | |
| 08:11:31.3656 | `S_RESET` `*` ×2 (Boot() constructor) | +37 ms |
| 08:11:34.8359 | `S_RUN` `+` (Boot.Loop) | +3.470 s |
| 08:11:36.6708 | `MCU verified: // H1S1 DSP` | S_RUN **+1.835 s** |
| 08:11:36.6892 | `// H1S4 SW Left` | +18.5 ms |
| 08:11:36.7091 | `// H1S3 SW Right` | +19.8 ms |
| 08:11:42.8368 | verdict | S_RUN **+8.001 s** |

The verdict fires 8.0 s after `S_RUN` and the last announce lands at 1.87 s:
**6.1 s of slack**. Widening the window buys nothing, and B13's "restart it
twice" is, as S27-6 already said, another roll of the same dice.

The **18.5 ms / 19.8 ms** spacing between the three logged lines repeats to
better than 1 ms on all seven clean restarts. That constancy is the clue §4
picks up.

## 3. Gate 2 — is anybody talking?

`matrix-app` stopped (`sudo systemctl stop matrix-app` — the app is the only
holder of `/dev/ttyAMA0`; `dsp4_bootloop.sh`'s stop/start was not used
because its `start` also drives `.ldr` images into the SHARCs, which this
session is barred from). Port opened raw, 8N1, **115200 baud** — read out of
`Boot.cs:117-122` / `Constants.SERIAL_BAUD_RATE`, not guessed.

### 3.1 Nobody announces unprompted

60 s passive on a fresh app-side open, nothing transmitted: **no announcement
of any kind**. The bus carries only MH1's running-mode heartbeat — `.` and
`:` alternating every ~253 ms, 237 lines in 60 s. The announce is a reply,
not a spontaneous emission, and opening the port does not provoke it.

### 3.2 Replaying what the app sends — all three answer, every time

The app's opening sequence exactly: `S_RESET` `*` twice, the measured 3.47 s
gap, then `S_RUN` `+`. **14 trials with the port to themselves, 3 of 3 on
every one**, and the timing is millisecond-stable across all of them:

```
  +0.003 s   // resuming normal operation
  +0.005 s   // debug only
  +0.208 s   // H1S1 DSP
  +0.211 s   // H1S4 SW Left
  +0.215 s   // H1S3 SW Right
```

**The whole announce burst is three lines inside 7 ms, 208 ms after `S_RUN`.**
Per-MCU latency: H1S1 208 ms, H1S4 211 ms, H1S3 215 ms — a fixed order and a
fixed ~3 ms spacing. This is the measurement S27 and S29 never took.

## 4. The app reads its own bus 5× slower than the bus delivers it

This is proved without any assumption about scheduling, from two lines the
app itself timestamps.

`// resuming normal operation` and `// debug only` are **2 ms apart on the
wire** (+0.003 and +0.005). In the app's own trace, on the failing restart:

```
11/09/2026 08:11:00.708267: Eol: ... // resuming normal operation
11/09/2026 08:11:00.725723: Eol: ... // debug only
```

**17.5 ms apart.** Two lines that are provably 2 ms apart on the wire take
17.5 ms to come out of the app, so the 18.5/19.8 ms announce spacing in §2.1
is host-side and not bus scheduling.

The mechanism is in the source, and it is structural:

- `GetRxData()` — the app's only serial reader — is called from exactly one
  place, `UiTimerCallback` → `Dispatcher.UIThread.Post` (`Boot.cs:780`). It
  runs on the **Avalonia UI thread**, behind a reentrancy guard.
- The timer that drives it is started at the **end** of `Boot.Loop()`
  (`Boot.cs:558`), **after** `S_RUN` has already been written (`Boot.cs:517`).
  Nothing is draining the port while the announce is on the wire.
- Each matched line costs a synchronous `AppContext.log` file append on that
  same thread. The trace shows the cost directly: `TxRxData SLOW: 9.78ms`,
  `17.18ms`, `19.38ms`.

So **every announce the app has ever seen was read out of a backlog**, during
the busiest 2 s of application startup. `Boot.cs:545` already half-knows this
— `WaitForResume()` is disabled with the comment "MCU sends the string after
boot data fills the buffer, which drains too slowly and exceeds the 3 s
timeout".

This is a real defect and it belongs to mx26, but §5 shows it is **not** what
loses the announce.

## 5. Gate 4 — the mechanism, named: H1S1 and H1S4 collide

Deferring the host's first read reproduces the fault **with `matrix-app`
stopped and no app in the path**: open the port, `S_RESET` ×2, 3.47 s reading
nothing, `S_RUN`, 1 s reading nothing, then drain for 8 s.

**40 trials, 6 failures — 15 %**, against the app ladder's 1 in 8 (12.5 %).
The rates agree, and the app is not in the path.

### 5.1 Grade one, 5 of the 6 — the collision

What comes back is not a missing line. It is a **garbled** one:

```
0000  2f 2f 20 72 65 73 75 6d 69 6e 67 20 6e 6f 72 6d  |// resuming norm|
0010  61 6c 20 6f 70 65 72 61 74 69 6f 6e 0a 0a 2f 2f  |al operation..//|
0020  20 64 65 62 75 67 20 6f 6e 6c 79 0a 3a 0a 2f 2f  | debug only.:.//|
0030  20 48 31 50 00 00 10 22 01 9a 5d 81 4c 65 66 74  | H1P..."..].Left|
0040  0a 2f 2f 20 48 31 53 33 20 53 57 20 52 69 67 68  |.// H1S3 SW Righ|
0050  74 0a 2e 0a 3a 0a ...                            |t...:...        |
```

`// H1S1 DSP\n// H1S4 SW Left\n` is 28 bytes. What arrived is `// H1`, then
**nine bytes of framing wreckage**, then `Left\n` — 19 bytes. Two
transmitters drove the line at once, the receiver lost framing part-way
through, and H1S3 — 4 ms further on — came through clean. The comment-line
count drops 5 → 4 and the trial reads **1 of 3, H1S3 only**.

**All five collisions garble exactly H1S1+H1S4 and leave H1S3 intact.** That
is S27-6's "H1S1 and H1S4 fail together or not at all" **confirmed and
explained**: they do not fail together by coincidence, they fail together
because they collide with each other. H1S1 announces at +208 ms and H1S4 at
+211 ms — **3 ms apart, against ~1.2 ms for one 14-character line at
115200**. There is no margin in that.

`SRX`/`MRX` are multi-drop by design — `MW/D24/HW/hardware-map.md` §3a: the
S/BUSY/SRX/MRX nets "are SHARED with U8 (M MCU) and enter LOGIC (U3)", and
the harness at J3/J4 takes them off-card to the matrix system. Several
devices drive one net into the host, with no arbitration on it.

### 5.2 Grade two, 1 of the 6 — the bus wedges mid-burst

The sixth failure is different. `resuming` and `debug only` arrive and then
**nothing at all — not even the heartbeat**:

```
trial 17: 0 of 3 []   rx=47B
0000  2f 2f 20 72 65 73 75 6d 69 6e 67 20 6e 6f 72 6d  |// resuming norm|
0010  61 6c 20 6f 70 65 72 61 74 69 6f 6e 0a 0a 2f 2f  |al operation..//|
0020  20 64 65 62 75 67 20 6f 6e 6c 79 0a 3a 0a        | debug only.:.|
```

47 bytes in an 8 s drain against 160 on a clean trial, and MH1's `.`/`:`
running-mode heartbeat — every ~253 ms on a healthy bus — **stops dead**. The
collision does not merely corrupt the announce; it can take the bus master
down with it.

**This is the grade the app's failing restart shows**, one announce further
in: H1S1 logged clean, then no further `//` line of any kind. `Eol()` traces
every `//` line that matches no MCU and the trace has exactly two
(`resuming`, `debug only`) and no third — so nothing arrived after H1S1, not
even wreckage. And it is the grade **S29 met five times**: 0 of 3, app alive,
bus quiet.

### 5.3 Why the surviving slave changes identity

Two grades, two signatures:

| grade | what happens | verdict | seen by |
|---|---|---|---|
| collision | H1S1 and H1S4 overlap, H1S3 clears | **1 of 3, H1S3** | S27-6 (×2), wire ×5 |
| wedge | bus stops mid-burst | **1 of 3 H1S1, or 0 of 3** | S31 restart 7, S29 (×5) |

So "H1S3 is never the one that fails" was a two-sample artefact of having met
only the collision grade.

### 5.4 The fix, and where it lives

**MCU firmware** — `mcu/H1S1/`, `mcu/H1S3/`, `mcu/H1S4/` in the fw tree.
Either stagger the replies to `S_RUN` by more than one line-time (**≥5 ms**
with margin, against today's 3 ms), or have MH1 poll the slaves individually
instead of broadcasting and letting all three answer at once. Nothing in the
app can fix it and no CPLD or wiring change is called for — the net is
multi-drop by design and the firmware is using it without arbitration. The
hub marks any mod; none is proposed here.

## 6. Gate 3 — what differs between S28 (3 of 3) and S29 (0 of 3)

**Nothing on the unit does. S28 and S29 differ by which face of a 15 % dice
they saw — and the evidence S29 quoted never supported the reading it was
quoted for.**

### 6.1 No power cycle was needed, and none had happened

`uptime` read 4:25 at 08:06 BST, i.e. the unit booted at **03:40 BST**, which
**predates the S29 dispatch (05:05Z)**. S29 ran entirely inside this uptime
and so does this session. The cheapest discriminator gate 3 nominates was
therefore already spent before the question was asked: the same power-on that
carried S29's 0-of-3 carries this session's 7-of-8.

### 6.2 The CPLD is not the difference

From the unit's own `sudo` audit, every CPLD flash since boot:

```
04:06:14  dsp4_logic_driveall.e13b5dec84e0
05:47:49  dsp4_logic.a1f6672af6c3        (shipping)
06:12:22  dsp4_logic_driveall.e13b5dec84e0
07:19:36  dsp4_logic_maincap.d903ae1ac4a9
07:37:25  dsp4_logic_pisel.bd9c100db7c2
07:42:35  dsp4_logic.a1f6672af6c3        (shipping)
```

S29's restarts (05:50:54, 05:53:20, 05:55:37, 05:55:58) all ran with the
**shipping** bitstream already loaded at 05:47:49 — the same bitstream loaded
now. The MH/panel UART copper does cross this CPLD (`dsp4_logic.qsf`:18-31
names `MHRX/MHTX/SRX/MRX/PTRX/S5-7/BUSY` as the pins a mis-built bitstream
ground-drives, hardware-proven 2026-08-19), so the question was a fair one —
but the timeline answers it no.

### 6.3 The sink S29 read cannot carry the evidence

S29's conclusion — 0 of 3, five times — names a real state of this unit
(§5.2 reproduces it on the wire). Its evidence does not reach it. S29 records
that the app "holds `/dev/ttyAMA0`, and **its journal carries no MCU text at
all**".

`journalctl -u matrix-app` carries **no MCU text on any restart, ever**. For
the whole of 2026-09-11, across the seven restarts this session that each
verified 3 of 3, `journalctl -u matrix-app | grep -icE "MCU verified|MCU boot
verified|not verified"` returns **0**. The app writes those lines to
`/home/app/logs/log` alone. An absence was read off an instrument that cannot
show a presence; the conclusion happened to be right and the evidence never
supported it.

Five 0-of-3 restarts in a row against a 15 % per-restart rate is 1 in 13,000,
so the wedge grade evidently **clusters** rather than being independent per
restart — once the bus is in that state it tends to stay there. That is
itself a fact for the firmware, and it is why S28 (3 of 3) and S29 (0 of 3)
can sit one day apart on an unchanged unit.

### 6.4 And the verdict lands later than S29's app instances lived

The verdict fires at `S_RUN` + 8.0 s, and `S_RUN` goes out 3.5 s after
service start: **15.3–16.3 s after start** on this unit across the eight
restarts. S29's app instances were stopped 18 s, 18 s and 27 s after start,
so that part is marginal rather than impossible — but any read before
~T+17 s reads zero by construction, and S27-6's method note (the log is
rewritten at app start) is only half the trap. The other half is that it is
also written *late*.

## 7. For the hub — the D24 attach

`AN_EN` is gated on **H1S1 alone**: `AnalogBringUp.ClockGates` fails the
"S MCU (H1S1) alive" gate unless `H1S1` is in `_verifiedMcus`, and
`RunAnalogBringUp()` runs from the post-`S_RUN` verification continuation
(`Boot.cs:540-543`).

**On this session's ladder, 8 of 8 restarts verified H1S1** — the one failing
restart still had it — so the attach is not blocked at the measured rate. But
that is luck of the grade, not a property of H1S1: the **collision grade
destroys H1S1's announcement outright** (5 of 5 wire failures did exactly
that), and had those landed on the app the gate would have refused and the
analog board would not have come up. On the wire, **H1S1 was lost in 5 of 40
trials — 12.5 %**.

So: **the attach can proceed today, and it will fail to raise AN_EN on
roughly one power-up in eight until the firmware is fixed.** That is a
decision for PW, not a blocker this repo can clear.

Two changes, neither of them this repo's to make:

1. **MCU firmware (the fix).** Stagger the slave replies to `S_RUN` by more
   than one line-time — ≥5 ms apart at 115200 — or poll the slaves
   individually from MH1. Today they answer at +208, +211, +215 ms, and the
   first two are 3 ms apart.

2. **App (mx26, `src/sw/app/Core/Boot.cs`) — the paragraph for the hub.**
   The app's only serial reader is started after the thing it must not miss.
   `Boot.Loop()` writes `S_RUN` at line 517 and starts the UI timer that
   drives `GetRxData()` at line 558; between those two points nothing drains
   `/dev/serial0`, and when draining does begin it runs on the Avalonia UI
   thread at one line per tick with a synchronous log append per line —
   measured at 17.5 ms for two lines that are 2 ms apart on the wire. Start
   the reader (or a dedicated reader thread that owns the port and feeds a
   queue) **before** `S_RUN` is written, and take the file append off the read
   path. That does not fix the collision, but it removes the app from the
   list of suspects and it makes the announce arrive in 210 ms instead of
   1.84 s — which matters directly, because the analog bring-up is gated on
   this verification and currently waits on a backlog.

No change is proposed to the 8 s window: it has 6.1 s of slack and widening
it addresses nothing.

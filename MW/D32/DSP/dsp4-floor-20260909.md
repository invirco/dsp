## 1. The floor, measured — and there was no document to read instead

**The ADSP-2156x SHARC+ Hardware Reference (Rev 1.0, December 2020) has no
core chapter at all.** Zero occurrences of "pipeline stage" or
"Instruction Pipeline" in its 101,300 lines; every "stall" in it is
peripheral flow control (SPI `RDY`, CRC, PKA, the DMA FIFO); every
"latency" near "compute" is a memory or bus latency. It states neither the
core's pipeline depth nor how many cycles a compute result takes before a
dependent instruction may read it. That information would be in the
SHARC+ Instruction Set / Programming Reference, which is not on this
machine. **The part is the document, so the part was asked.**

`bq_probe.asm` runs 24 rungs of ONE loop nest — 28 outer × 15 inner, the
pipelined biquad kernel's own trip counts — whose inner body is the only
thing that differs. Same subroutine shape, same register seeding, same
pointer reset in every rung, so the deltas are clean. Minimum of five
repeats, 32 calls each.

### The 1.47 is not a rate. It is four avoidable stalls.

- **Instructions issue at exactly 1.000 cycles each.** The nop ladder is
  2/3/5/8 → 2.402/3.402/5.402/8.402; the slope is 1.000 and the intercept,
  0.402 c/iteration, is what a 15-trip hardware loop costs on top of its
  body.
- **A COMPUTE result is not readable by the next instruction.** Every
  producer→consumer edge between ADJACENT instructions costs exactly one
  extra cycle, and the cost is identical for every unit pairing:
  **mul→mul 0.984, ALU→ALU 0.984, mul↔ALU 0.984**. Four independent
  operations cost 4.402 and four dependent ones 8.338.
- **A memory LOAD's result IS readable by the next instruction, free.**
  That is why the old loop's measured stall count is 2 and not 3: its
  `f0 = dm(i2,0)` → multiply pair is not a stall.
- **The DM bus is not the constraint.** The shipping body's two memory
  moves cost **0.009 cycles**. Deleting them changes nothing.
- **The SIMD pair is free.** PEYEN on versus off: **0.000 cycles**.
- **The PM bus is a pessimisation.** The same store over PM instead of DM
  costs **+1.068 cycles**. Do not move biquad data to PM.
- **Short loops are nearly free.** A 15-trip loop against a 60-trip one
  carrying the same total iterations: 0.148 c/iteration.
- **The real per-stage prologue, preamble, peeled sample and state
  write-back** are 2.213 c/iteration over 15 samples = **33.2 cycles per
  stage**, which is what reconciles this rig with the graph ladder's
  5,655 c/blk.

So the shipping body is **5 issue + 4 stalls (I1→I2, I2→I3, I3→I4, and
I5's f15 across the back edge)** and the old one **8 issue + 2 stalls**.
That is the whole of S13-5's disappointing result: the 8→5 rewrite removed
three instructions and bought back two stalls, for a net 1.073 cycles.
**"1.47 cycles per instruction" was never a rate.**

## 2. THE DYNAMICS — the one table wins, and the gate is not a table problem

`dyn_shootout.asm`, 14 rungs of the same loop nest, the same envelope and
the same gain application in every one, so what differs is the gain
computer. Cycles per SAMPLE for a PAIR of channels; the per-channel column
is half. Minimum of five repeats.

| rung | c/sample-pair | c/sample/channel |
|---|--:|--:|
| envelope + attack/release one-pole | 17.0 | 8.5 |
| **the gain computer TODAY, 6-term polynomials** | **215.2** | **107.6** |
| the same, 3-term polynomials | 149.1 | 74.6 |
| **ONE level→gain table, gather over DM** | **49.1** | **24.5** |
| the same, gather over DM and PM in one instruction | 55.0 | 27.5 |
| the same, whole computer unpaired | 67.0 | 33.5 |
| `LOG2Q_SIMD` alone — what the paired GATE pays today | 73.1 | 36.5 |
| `MRF_RNS28_SIMD` alone | 15.0 | 7.5 |
| **the WHOLE compressor per-sample body, today** | **284.3** | **142.2** |
| **the same, with the level→gain LUT** | **118.1** | **59.1** |
| **the WHOLE gate per-sample body, today** | **142.1** | **71.1** |
| **the same, LINEAR-domain threshold** | **65.0** | **32.5** |
| the same, level→gain LUT | 114.1 | 57.1 |

### The compressor: 142.2 → 59.1 cycles per sample per channel

The one table takes **166.1 cycles per sample-pair out of the gain
computer, 77 % of it**, and **58.5 % out of the whole compressor body**.
The reduced-order polynomial — the contender with no gather at all — is
only 31 % and loses outright. PW's accuracy ruling turns out to buy the
fastest option rather than trade against it.

### The gather is worth paying for, and the pairing survives it

Both channels' indices leave SIMD in **one store**, because a
direct-address store inside a PEYEN region writes PEy's word right after
PEx's — the same property that makes `_bqfl_two` a pair. The four gathered
words come back in **two paired reads** for the same reason. PEy's
registers are undisturbed while PEYEN is down (only the compute unit is
idle), so the interpolation fraction survives the excursion without a
spill.

- Dropping the pairing entirely (PW's option ii) costs **18.0 cycles
  more** than the excursion does.
- Routing the second channel over the **PM bus** costs **6.0 cycles
  more** rather than saving the two instructions it removes — the same PM
  penalty the biquad probe measured on a store, now measured on a read.

**Recommendation: keep the pairing, gather over DM, take the PEYEN
excursion.**

### The GATE is a step, not a curve — do not table it

Tabling the gate's static curve is **worse than today** (114.1 against
142.1) where the linear-domain threshold gives **65.0**. The modelling
says why: the gate's curve is unity above threshold and `range` below, and
linear interpolation smears a discontinuity across a whole cell whatever
the mesh — the worst error stays at **52–60 dB at every point count from
2 to 64 points per octave**. No table fixes a step.

`log2(env) ≥ thr` is `env ≥ 2^thr`. `DSP4_GATE_LINTHR` has done exactly
that conversion once per block in the SCALAR kernel since S8, where it was
worth 101 cycles/sample. `dsp_codegen.py`'s paired-graph header `#error`s
on it because the two are different arithmetic — by **at most a 0.0002 dB
threshold shift**, a fixed offset and not per-sample noise. That was worth
arguing about against a 0.0001 dB polynomial. Against a **0.1 dB ruling it
is not.**

**Recommendation: port `DSP4_GATE_LINTHR` into the paired GATE. It is
77.1 cycles per sample-pair, 54 % of the gate body, and the code already
exists and is proven scalar.**

### The table itself — points, error, rebuild, memory

Modelled host-side (the rig cannot see error: the table's contents do not
change the instruction stream).

Index = exponent from `leftz` + top *m* mantissa bits, so the table is
**log-spaced by construction** and the index costs a count-leading-zeros,
two shifts and an add — a logarithm without a logarithm.

| *m* | pts/octave | words (17 octaves) | hard-knee COMP | soft-knee COMP | LIMITER |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 2 | 4 | 69 | 0.300 | 0.121 | 0.328 |
| 3 | 8 | 137 | 0.149 | 0.030 | 0.161 |
| 4 | 16 | 273 | 0.120 | 0.010 | 0.086 |
| 5 | 32 | 545 | 0.052 | 0.003 | 0.053 |

Uniform spacing needs *m* = 5 to clear 0.1 dB. **Anchoring a knot exactly
at the threshold and at both knee corners buys two levels of *m*:** at
*m* = 3 with anchors, hard-knee COMP, soft-knee COMP and LIMITER all land
at **0.030 dB**. And because the design step knows the threshold, density
is only needed for about two octaves below it — everything further down is
exactly flat at unity, where interpolation error is zero.

With adaptive placement at *m* = 3 + anchors: **COMP 19–101 words,
LIMITER 20–47 words, 0.030 dB in every case.** At the shipped defaults
(COMP thr −20 dB ratio 4 knee 6; LIMITER thr −0.5 dB) it is **49 and 20
words** — inside PW's 50–75 band with ~3× margin under the bar. Across the
full documented parameter sweep (thr to −60 dB, ratio to 100:1) the worst
node wants ~100 words; **50–75 clears the bar only for thresholds above
about −30 dB.**

**Quadratic interpolation does not help** (0.102 dB against linear's
0.120 dB at *m* = 4): the hard-knee corner is a kink, not a smooth
function, and quadratic convergence assumes smoothness. The corner is
fixed by placing a knot on it, not by raising the interpolation order.

**The design step is not affordable per block.** A full level→gain
evaluation is ~211 instructions per point, so a 50-word rebuild is ~10,550
cycles and a 100-word one ~21,100, against a 16-sample block's 327,680
cycles at the clock the instrument reads — 3 % and 6 % of a whole block for
ONE node. **For a ramping parameter, rebuild the target table once when
the ramp starts and blend two tables per sample** (~65–75 instructions per
sample-pair, still far below today's 211); do not rebuild per block.

**Memory is the open risk.** 96 dynamics nodes (GATE 36, COMP 42, LIMITER
18) each need their own table because their parameters differ:
**~2,420 words at shipped defaults, ~5,000 at worst-case parameters.**
`dyn_tables_fx.asm`'s header records that a **1,024-word** pair already
overflowed `sec_stak` and had to be halved to 514, so this does not fit in
DM and would have to go to L2. **The rig measured a DM-resident table; an
L2-resident one is NOT measured, and a per-sample gather out of L2 is a
different instrument.** That is the first thing the integration session
must settle, because it can move the 49.1.

### The LIMITER's 4,775 c/blk, explained

`PAIR_AUX_LIM` calls `_lim_pair_blk` (`src/lib2/lim_simd_fx.asm`), whose
per-sample body is ≈251 instructions for the pair:

| stage | instr/sample-pair |
|---|--:|
| envelope + attack/release (one combined one-pole) | 17 |
| **log2 (`LOG2Q_SIMD`, 13 + `POLYQ_SIMD` 60)** | **73** |
| gain computer / knee (both candidates computed, then selected) | 49 |
| **exp2 (`EXP2Q_SIMD`, 29 + `POLYQ_SIMD` 60)** | **89** |
| gain application (`dry*gain`, one round) | 18 |
| fetch/store + pipeline pad | 5 |

**log2 + exp2 are 162 of 251, about 65 %** — and the reason a class "with
no cascade in it" is bigger than the EQ and AFB pairs together is
structural, not accidental: the paired kernel is *conditional compute*.
The scalar kernel branches around `exp2` on any sample below threshold;
the SIMD kernel cannot, because two channels may want different arms, so
`COMPGAIN_SIMD` computes both `LOG2Q_SIMD` and `EXP2Q_SIMD`
unconditionally and selects afterwards. Under `sigprofile2.sh` (chip 1
injects a −6 dBFS square wave that crosses into chip 2, so the bus is not
silent) the scalar equivalent would have skipped `exp2` on essentially
every sample. **The one table removes the branch problem along with the
polynomials: there is nothing left to be conditional about.**

The GATE's linear-domain precedent does not transfer to COMP or LIMITER —
they need the log VALUE for the knee, not just a comparison — which is
exactly why the level→gain table, and not a log2 table, is the answer.

**What ADI publishes on SHARC dynamics, checked:** nothing current with
source. The 1998 ADSP-21065L "Digital Audio Effects" EZ-KIT carried
assembly compressor/expander/limiter; SigmaStudio(+) dynamics modules ship
as binaries. There is no vendor reference implementation to compare
against.

## 3. The primitive re-derived — six instructions that do not stall beat
five that do

With the rule measured, the binding resource on the float SIMD biquad is
neither the instruction count nor `max(multiplies, ALU, moves)`. It is the
**loop-carried dependence chain**: y → q1 → w1′ → y, three dependent
operations at two cycles an edge = **six cycles per sample per stage**,
against five instructions to issue. Six slots is the exact floor for a
schedule that keeps the arithmetic, and `DSP4_BQ_SIMD_PIPE=2` reaches it:

```
S1  p0 = x*b0    w1 = t  - q1
S2  p1 = x*b1    w2 = p2 - q2
S3  p2 = x*b2    y  = w1 + p0
S4                t = w2 + p1     load  x(n+1)
S5  q1 = y*a1                     store y(n)
S6  q2 = a2*y
```

Every dependence is at least two slots deep; nothing is adjacent.
Registers are placed for the dual-compute operand quadrants (multiplier X
from R0–R3 and Y from R4–R7, ALU X from R8–R11 and Y from R12–R15), with
a2 in R0–R3 because S6 is the one multiply with no ALU partner and a
standalone multiply is unrestricted — the same constraint and the same
answer as the loop it replaces.

| | c/sample/stage, the pair | | c/iter with the real per-stage code |
|---|--:|---|--:|
| the OLD 8-instruction loop | 10.415 | = 8 issue + 2 stalls | — |
| the 5-instruction loop (PIPE=1) | 9.343 | = 5 issue + 4 stalls | 11.556 |
| **THE SIX-SLOT LOOP (PIPE=2)** | **6.409** | **= 6 issue + 0 stalls** | **8.620** |

6.409 against a predicted 6.402 is the model confirmed to a tenth of a
percent, and 1.068 c/instruction says it does not stall anywhere.

**Bit-exact, proved twice.** Off the part,
`tools/dsp/bq_simd_pipe_check.py` now executes all THREE schedules
register by register over 4,000 random cascades × 4 consecutive blocks ×
16 samples: **zero mismatches** in the outputs and in both final state
words, pointer advance exactly BLOCK on i2 and i3. On the part,
`bqeverify float`: **BQE_VERIFY PASS, 0 ULP against `bq_float_ref` over
36,864 words, hash `0xC607BA6B` sum `0xE67685E7`** — the same hash the
unpipelined and five-slot kernels produce — with the offset reconstruction
still live on 567 of 576 cells exactly as the model names them.

**The control is inert:** built at `DSP4_BQ_SIMD_PIPE=0` the pair is
`df6b847d` / `cb9bc58e`, byte for byte the staged `geq_*` pair. Default
stays 0.

**Below six means changing the arithmetic.** Folding B1 = b1 − a1·b0 and
B2 = b2 − a2·b0 at design time shortens the recurrence to two operations
(w1 → a1·w1 → w1′) for a four-cycle floor, at which point five
instructions would be issue-bound and reachable. It is a different
rounding sequence, so it is **not** bit-exact; it is named as the next
lever and needs a ULP bound and a ruling before it is built.

## 4. RIG B — the 2156x IIR accelerator: 32-bit FAILS, 40-bit PASSES

Read out of the HRM chapter 35 and modelled against the repo's own band
design (`tools/dsp/geq_ref.py::design_band`, checked bit-identical to
`bq_float_ref.rbj_peak`), fs = 48 kHz, band 1 f0 = 19.9526 Hz, band 2
f0 = 25.1189 Hz, Q = 4.318473.

**The accelerator is a transposed direct form II biquad** — the same form
this tree's float cascade already uses — in **32-bit or 40-bit IEEE
floating point**, selectable per channel (`IIR_SGCTL.FORTYBIT`), rounding
to nearest-even or truncating (`RND`), with coefficients stored
**pre-negated** (Ak = b, Bk = −a) in the order Ak0, Ak1, Bk1, Ak2, Bk2,
Dk2, Dk1. It has **no saturation**: out-of-range results raise MAC
exceptions rather than clamping.

Pole geometry, which is the physical reason the test goes the way it does:

| band | gain | pole radius | 1 − r | (1 − r)/ε₃₂ |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 1 (19.95 Hz) | +12 dB | 0.99984845 | 1.515e−4 | 1271× |
| 1 | −12 dB | 0.99939682 | 6.032e−4 | 5060× |
| 2 (25.12 Hz) | +12 dB | 0.99980922 | 1.908e−4 | 1600× |
| 2 | −12 dB | 0.99924070 | 7.593e−4 | 6369× |

Worst magnitude error over f0/2 … 2·f0, by 21-point Goertzel sweep of the
actual sample-by-sample recursion (not an analytic H(z) evaluation — the
state recursion is where the precision goes):

| band | gain | binary32 | 40-bit extended |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| 1 | +12 dB | 0.215 dB | 0.00045 dB |
| 1 | −12 dB | 0.189 dB | 0.00136 dB |
| 2 | +12 dB | 0.061 dB | 0.00066 dB |
| 2 | −12 dB | 0.138 dB | 0.00071 dB |

**Verdict: band 1 at ±12 dB IS NOT inside the 0.01 dB bar in the
accelerator's 32-bit mode, and IS inside it in its 40-bit mode.**

The nominal headroom (1200–6400× ε₃₂) is real but is consumed by
compounding: the accelerator's table stores a1, a2 **directly**, with no
offset encoding to dodge the cancellation at a1 ≈ −1.9997, so a single
coefficient carries a float32 ulp of ≈2.4e−7 — 64× coarser than Q4.28's
absolute step — and that error compounds through 30,000–140,000 samples of
ring-down. The 40-bit format's extra eight mantissa bits restore the
margin by 256×. All four combinations are **stable** in every precision;
truncation showed no limit cycle or DC offset but is a biased rounding
mode with no consistent accuracy benefit, so **round-to-nearest is the
correct setting for audio**.

**Capacity is the harder limit.** The accelerator's coefficient memory is
1440 × 40 bits = **288 biquads device-wide**, and its state memory holds
288 biquads' worth — the per-channel "up to 64 biquads" and "32 channels"
figures are not simultaneously achievable. Chip 2's six aux GEQ pairs are
12 channels × 31 bands = **372 biquads, 84 more than the part has.** At
most **9 of the 12** channels could run on it at full 31 bands, or all
twelve would have to shrink to 24 bands.

So RIG B is **not closed but not a general answer either**: it would need
40-bit mode, it cannot take all the GEQ, and it buys nothing that §3 has
not already bought more cheaply and bit-exactly. It is not the next lever.

## 7. What S14 did not reach

**The dynamics work is measured and designed but NOT in the generator.**
No switch, no design step, no `famverify`, no `dyn_state_bound.py` re-run,
no capacity arm. That integration is one coherent piece — the design step
that bakes the curve, the per-node tables, the L2-vs-DM decision, the
ramping blend, the `DSP4_GATE_LINTHR` paired port, and the bit-exactness
bars — and splitting half of it off half-certified would be worse than
handing it over whole with the numbers it needs. Everything it needs to be
a one-shot session is in §2.

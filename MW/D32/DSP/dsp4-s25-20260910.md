# S25 — the pad test, the one pan table, and the D24 rows

Bench: rev C unit MW-D24-2, `app@192.168.1.219`. The control arm and the
S24 candidate are the same image, and the session opened by proving it:
a fresh build from `shipping.config.s21` reproduced `2fdd9f95` / `9222c2ee`
byte for byte, which is what the bench has staged as `s24_*`. No deploy;
`shipping.config` and `shipping.config.s21` unchanged.

---

## 0. What this session settled

1. **The pool alignment was never required.** S24 called the block pool's
   even placement "luck, not a declaration" and left it first in the queue.
   It is neither luck nor a requirement: the pools were moved onto ODD word
   addresses on both chips and **every audio bar reproduced bit for bit.**
   The net answer changes from 8 bytes to 4, and the 802.1Q caveat that was
   the only actionable item in `docs/net-contract-answers.md` **dissolves.**
2. **R5 is built and witnessed.** One 127-entry pan table per law, `Pan` an
   index, the law selected at block rate by the system cell, `Chan*LcrOn`
   selecting the three-column read and `Chan*CtrOn` gating the centre leg —
   **with no new bus, no new inter-chip lane and no TDM slot**, because on
   this product the sub bus IS the centre bus and always was. 20 of 20
   readings bit-exact on the part, and the pre-R5 audio reproduces byte for
   byte.
3. **`fix` on this core ROUNDS, it does not truncate** — measured, against
   what this tree's own comments say in three places. It cost the pan
   table one wrong build and it is the kind of shared belief that is worth
   more than the bug it caused.

## 1. Gate 1 — the pad test, and the answer is 4 bytes

### 1.1 The instrument

`DSP4_POOL_PAD` emits N words of `.var` immediately in front of
`_blk_pool`, in the same section and the same object. A slot is
`base + n × BLOCK` and BLOCK is 16, so the parity of every slot in the pool
is the parity of its base: an ODD N moves the whole pool. It is emitted by
the generator into `bus_accumulators.asm` (that file is generated —
`gen_bus_accumulators_fixed` — so a hand edit would have survived until the
next codegen run and then vanished), declared in `dsp_block.h`, and passed
by `build.sh` like any other build flag. **0 emits nothing at all**, and the
control arm proves it: `DSP4_POOL_PAD=0` rebuilt `2fdd9f95` / `9222c2ee`,
byte for byte.

| | control | pad arm (`DSP4_POOL_PAD=1`) |
|---|---|---|
| chip 1 `_blk_pool` / `_blk_pool1` | `0x90330` / `0x903C0` — EVEN | `0x90331` / `0x903C1` — **ODD** |
| chip 2 `_blk_pool` / `_blk_pool1` | `0x91E74` / `0x91F04` — EVEN | `0x91E75` / `0x91F05` — **ODD** |
| `chip1.ldr` / `chip2.ldr` | `2fdd9f95` / `9222c2ee` | `f232870f` / `2f96e555` |

Both pools, both chips, off the even address the linker has handed them on
every build on record.

### 1.2 The bars, and they do not move

| bar | control | pad arm |
|---|---|---|
| `busgold`, 256-word main-bus capture | sha256 `4126c00730a31f5f` | **`4126c00730a31f5f` — identical** |
| `goldnode` — GATE, COMP, TUBE, FDR | see below | **word-for-word identical** |
| `famverify` D32, 26 families | 21 LIVE, 3 numeric BIT_EXACT | **verdict-for-verdict and value-for-value identical** |
| golden harness | 59/59 | 59/59 |
| `dsp_validate` | OK, 698 nodes, the same four pre-existing process-order notes | the same |

Three of those need a word about what they are and are not.

**`busgold` reports GRAPH DIFFERS on both arms, and that is not the pad.**
The stored golden is `busgraph-postD59-20260830.json`, taken on
`shipping.config`; `shipping.config.s21` turns on DYN_LUT, GATE_LINTHR and
C2_BQ_GRAPH, all of which change the audio by design. 235 of 256 words
differ, first at word 21, maxdiff 303,276 — **and the two arms produce the
same 235 words, the same first index and the same maxdiff, from the same
sha256.** The bar that matters here is arm-against-arm, and it is exact.

**`goldnode` reports NODE VERIFY FAILED on both arms, and that is not the
pad either.** COMP's step arm reads `0 of 96 bit-exact` with a worst
deviation of **0.00518 dB** against `fixed_ref` — the level→gain LUT arm
scored against the polynomial model, well inside `dyn_lut_design.py`'s
documented 0.0950 dB, and a pre-existing property of this configuration.
GATE, TUBE and FDR are 96 of 96 bit-exact on both arms. Every captured
word from the part — including the deviating COMP arm's — is identical
between the two arms, which is the comparison the pad test is.

**`famverify`'s one difference is a live meter word.** Stripping run
metadata, the two 26-family reports differ in exactly one field: METER's
`meter_word`, `0x3E8FA7C8` against `0x3E8D4B3B` (0.2512 vs 0.2483). That
family's verdict is `NO_PROBE` / `NO_VERDICT` in both — it is a
free-running reading of live signal taken a few seconds apart, not a bar.

### 1.3 Why it does not move, which is the half that makes it a result

A measurement that cannot be explained is a measurement waiting to be
contradicted. This one has a mechanism.

The 8-byte argument was that the block kernels open a `PEYEN` region and
read the pool with `dm(i0, 2)`, and that a SIMD dual-data access needs an
even base. But `src/lib/dyn_simd_fx.asm` says in its own header what that
access actually does — *"a data access reads two consecutive words — PEx
the addressed one, PEy the next"* — the addressed one, whatever its parity.
The access class that WOULD require an even address is the **long word**,
and **this tree contains none: `LW(` appears zero times in every `.asm` in
the source tree.** There is also not a single `.align` directive anywhere,
which is consistent — nothing has ever needed one.

So the pools have been landing even because the linker happened to, and
nothing was ever leaning on it.

### 1.4 What it changes for the net spoke

`docs/net-contract-answers.md` §2 is corrected: **4 bytes, set by the DMA**
(`MSIZE04` / `XMOD 4`), not 8 set by the core. `MWN_AUDIO_PAYLOAD_OFF`
**stays at 40** either way — but §2.3, the one thing in that document the
net spoke had to act on, is **retired**. With an 8-byte requirement a
single 802.1Q tag from a customer switch moved the header 4 bytes and
flipped the alignment; with a 4-byte requirement a 4-byte shift preserves
it. There is no receiver-side placement rule to declare and no tagged-frame
copy to pay for.

The old §2.2 and §2.3 are left in place, marked, rather than deleted. The
reasoning that was wrong is worth reading beside the measurement that
settled it — and the net spoke was told the 8-byte answer on 2026-09-10, so
withdrawing it has to be visible.

## 2. Gate 2 — R5, the one pan table

### 2.1 The thing R5 needed that nobody had checked: where the centre goes

R5 puts every strip on one 127 × (gL, gC, gR) table. The obvious objection
is that a **centre leg needs a centre bus**, and chip 1's fabric is Main L,
Main R, Sub, 4 groups, 12 aux, 6 FX and the matrix rows — no centre. The
four "main outputs" are the CROSSOVER's four outputs, not four buses.
Building an LCR centre would then mean a 33rd fabric row, an inter-chip
lane, a TDM slot out of the single-sourced slot map and a chip-2 chain, on
the chip S24 measured at 100.72 % at worst use. That would have been a
refusal.

It is not needed. **`Chan*CtrOn` is already dispatched to `_rtg_sub_on`,
and the master's own name for the cell is "Center/sub output assign
on/off".** On this product the sub bus IS the centre bus, with a complete
chain behind it — `C1_BUS_SUB` → send → `C2_RECV_SUB` → fader → EQ → comp →
limiter → delay → its own TDM output. The destination has been in the graph
since the graph existed, and R5's "`Chan*CtrOn` still GATES the centre leg"
is a description of a crosspoint that is already there.

So R5 costs **no fabric row, no inter-chip lane and no TDM slot**, and
R6–R11's hold on fabric growth is not touched.

### 2.2 The column convention, and the defect in R5 as written

R5-amended says a non-LCR channel "uses the same table's L/R columns
(centre column ignored)". **Read literally that silences a centre-panned
stereo channel**: under hard LCR the centre position is *centre bus only*,
so its L and R legs are both zero. The same is true of the constant-power
law. "Ignored" cannot be what the columns hold, and this is worth PW seeing
rather than quietly working around.

What the table stores instead, which satisfies R5 literally and fixes it:

    column L, column R  =  what a NON-LCR channel reads, unmodified
    column C            =  the centre leg
    an LCR channel's legs = (L − C/2, C, R − C/2)

— the centre bus takes what the two sides carried between them, at −6 dB
each. The ordinary centre downmix, run backwards.

**For hard LCR this is an identity, not an approximation.** Hard LCR is
`p ≤ ½ : (1−2p, 2p, 0)` and `p ≥ ½ : (0, 2−2p, 2p−1)`. Fold the centre back
at half into each side and the sides become `(1−p, p)` for every p on both
halves — which is exactly the linear pan law this graph has always run. And
in the integer form the table actually stores it, the centre column is
**twice the smaller side**, so `C >> 1` gives the smaller side back and the
recovered legs are exact with no rounding at all: the near side becomes
`L − R` and the far side becomes **exactly zero**, at all 127 positions.

That is what makes "switching R5 on moves no non-LCR channel" a
construction rather than a hope, and `tools/dsp/pan_table.py::check()`
holds it at every index.

### 2.3 The one question R5 does not answer, carried not guessed

**Three columns cannot hold both a two-bus constant-power law and a
three-bus one.** That is arithmetic, not preference, and it was found by
trying: defining law 1's L/R columns as `cos(pπ/2) / sin(pπ/2)` (a proper
stereo constant-power law) makes the recovered LCR legs miss constant power
by up to 0.238 in sum-of-squares.

So law 1's PRIMARY definition is its LCR legs — "three-bus constant-power"
is a statement about three legs and R5 gives no other — and the stereo
projection is the −6 dB centre fold, whatever that comes to. It comes to a
peak of **+0.9687 dB above unity at index 107**, which is the ordinary
"the middle is louder in a downmix" bump, nothing saturates (Q4.28 reaches
8.0), and it is reported by `pan_table.fold_peak_db()` rather than
remembered. Under law 0 the same fold is the identity and the peak is
**+0.0000 dB**.

**What a non-LCR channel should read under the constant-power law is a
product question and is on PW's desk, not answered here.**

### 2.4 What was built

- **Two resident tables**, `_pan_tab_lcr` and `_pan_tab_cp`, 381 words each,
  `#if CHIP_ID == 1` — chip 2 does not pan and pays nothing. Generated from
  `tools/dsp/pan_table.py`, which is the law's single definition: the
  generator builds the words from it and the bench probe scores the part
  against it, so a disagreement is real and not two transcriptions drifting.
- **Both laws resident, the BASE selected**, rather than one copied in on a
  change. R5 asks for "one table swap at block rate"; a compare and a
  conditional register move is that swap with no copy, no transient, and no
  block in which half the strips have read the old law and half the new.
- **`_sys_lcr_law`**, one word for the whole desk, outside the
  `DSP4_PAN_TABLE` guard so the control arm links.
- **The read is ONE routine for all 32 strips** — `chip1/pan_law.asm`,
  generated — for `dyn_lut_fx.asm`'s reason and it is the same size of
  reason: inlined, the read cost **5,504 bytes** of chip 1's code pool
  (24,274 free → 18,770, 90.7 % → 92.8 %); factored, it costs **1,552**
  (24,274 → 22,722, 90.7 % → **91.3 %**). It is in `chip1/` and not
  `src/lib/`, because `src/lib` links into both chips and chip 2 would have
  paid program memory for a routine it can never call.
- **ROUTING's sub crosspoint** becomes `LcrOn ? _fdr_cq : unity`, still
  gated by `Chan*CtrOn`. LCR off is unity — exactly the sub send it has
  always been.
- **`Chan*LcrOn` (32 cells) and `Sys[1-1]LcrLaw[1-1]`** allocated after
  every other chip-1 address, the matrix sends' pattern, because the
  strip's 144-word page is exactly full (FADER_PAN 80–83, ROUTING 84–143).
  **Not one landed address moves.** The FADER_PAN block's fourth word is
  NOT reclaimed: PW reserved it on 2026-08-30 and reserved means reserved.
- **S22-4's trap, closed without a second handler range.** A write outside
  the 144-word page lands in the control-epoch catch-all no strip node
  watches — which is exactly how the matrix sends' crosspoint stayed at
  zero. FADER_PAN's block-rate body runs unconditionally so it sees the
  cells anyway; ROUTING's does not, so the fader raises `_fdr_busy` — the
  word that already means "ROUTING must re-prep" — for one block whenever
  the (LcrOn, law) pair moves. Five instructions a strip a block, and the
  SPI handler is untouched.

`Chan*LcrOn` is a **D32-only cell**: D24's master does not define it. D24
gets the table (one firmware, one address map) and no channel can turn LCR
on, so every D24 strip reads the L/R columns — which under law 0 is the pan
law it has today, word for word.

### 2.6 S25-2: `fix` rounds, and this tree has said otherwise for months

The probe's first run failed, and the failure was worth the run.

Pan indices 31, 63 and 94 came back as **32, 64 and 94**. The index step
was `fix(pan × 126 + 0.5)`, written with the `+0.5` on the belief — stated
in this tree's comments, in `dsp4_s24_probe.py::q28()`, and in the first
draft of `pan_table.py` — that **`fix` truncates toward zero**.

The three float32 products are exactly 31.0, 63.0 and 94.0, so the sums are
exactly 31.5, 63.5 and 94.5. Truncation gives 31/63/94. Round-half-away
gives 32/64/95. **Only round-half-to-even gives 32/64/94, and it gives all
three.** MODE1's truncate bit is set nowhere in the tree.

So `fix` rounds to nearest, ties to even. The `+0.5` was making every exact
index land on a tie and round up to the next one. It is gone; the model's
`_q428` rounds the same way; and `pan_table.check()` gained a round-trip
bar over all 127 indices that would have caught it on the desk.

**S24's probe could not have caught this and did not claim to**: its five
levels were 1.0, 0.5, 0.25, 0.0 and a mute, every one an exact power of two
at Q4.28, so no rounding was ever exercised. Its five-of-five result stands.
But `q28()`'s comment is wrong and so is every other place in the tree that
says `fix` truncates, and anything that has been converting a non-dyadic
constant is off by up to one LSB from what its comment claims.

## 4. Gate 4 — leg 2 continued, and what it found

The dispatch asked for anti-clip, the pick-offs / destinations and the RTA
"as the master names them". Three of the four turned out to be better
served by naming the deficiency correctly than by building an address, and
one is priced rather than built. Nothing here is guessed.

### 4.1 The destinations are a missing FAN-OUT, not a missing address (S25-5)

`Talk*Dest[2-3]` and `Noise*Dest[1-10]` carried reasons about SPI block
sizes. Those reasons were true and named the wrong deficiency.

**`C1_TALK_01`, `C1_TALK_02` and `C1_NOISE` all have an EMPTY outputs
column.** The talkback node already declares `_talk_route_<nid>[3]`, so the
words exist; **nothing in the tree reads that array beyond the SPI dispatch
of `Dest1`**. The mics are gained, high-passed, and delivered nowhere. The
noise generator has no route state at all.

So `Talk001Dest001` — which *does* reach an address — is a cell that
reaches a WORD and not the arithmetic. That is S24-7's shape one family
along, and `famverify`'s "TALKBACK contract 4/4, audio LIVE" cannot see it,
because the node itself runs perfectly well.

Adding the four missing addresses would have added four more such cells.
**They are deliberately not added.** What the families need is crosspoints
out of each node into named buses, and *which* buses is the master's
"aux/main" to resolve — three for each talkback, ten for the noise
generator. Priced against the fabric S24 measured: a live crosspoint on a
bus that already carries signal is below this instrument's resolution
(S24: five more on one bus cost < 0.1 points), so the cost is the SPI
words, the fan-out code, and nothing measurable in cycles. The unmapped
reasons now say all of that.

### 4.2 Anti-clip — the arithmetic is here twice, the decision is not

`Chan[1-32]AntiClip[1-1]`, "Auto gain reduction on/off (M&W exclusive)".
Not ruled by the addendum, and not built.

What is worth recording is that **the arithmetic already exists twice
over**: the COMPRESSOR node carries a `lim_mode` parameter, and the whole
level→gain LUT path (`DSP4_DYN_LUT`, S15/S16) is in every strip. A
brickwall at a fixed near-full-scale threshold is a table and a multiply
this graph already runs thirty-two times.

What is missing is the DECISION, and it is the kind that must not be
guessed. An anti-clip that reuses the channel compressor fights the desk's
own settings; one that does not is a second dynamics node per strip on the
chip whose worst-use row S24 measured at 100.72 %. Where it sits in the
strip and whether it is a limiter or a gain-ride are both PW's. The
unmapped reason says so.

### 4.3 RTA — priced by construction, not built

`Rta[1-1]On[1-1]` and `Rta[1-1]Src[1-1]`. A 1/3-octave analyser is 31
band-passes and 31 detectors on one selected source — a read-only meter
family and a NEW NODE CLASS.

It is priced before it is built rather than after, and the price needs no
guess because **this graph already runs exactly that arithmetic**: the GEQ
is a 31-band cascade and there are twelve of them on chip 2. One RTA
instance is one such cascade plus a per-band detector, on ONE source
instead of per aux — so it is roughly one twelfth of the measured chip-2
GEQ family, plus detectors.

It is **not built**, for a reason that is not cycles: `Rta*Src` selects
"main/aux/cue" and the master does not say which instances. A meter family
that reads the wrong point is worse than none, and the pick list is a
product question. Both reasons are now in `dsp-unmapped.csv`.

### 4.4 `Main*LimiterRng` — unchanged, and the existing reason is right

The LIMITER node carries On, Thr, Att and Rel and no range word
(`expand_limiter`, four SPI words). The reason on record already says
exactly that and nothing this session found improves it. Not ruled, not
built.

### 4.5 The pick-offs need nothing — R4 already closed them

`Chan*AuxPick*` and `Chan*FxPick*` implement all four positions in the
router and `Aux*PickOff` is host-managed, both settled by R4 in S24. There
was nothing here for this session to build, and it built nothing.

# S18 — the code pool's real lever, measured; and chip 2's ten points explained

Session S18, 2026-09-10. Bench: rev C unit, shipping CPLD `a1f6672af6c3`,
`dsp4-pcm-slave`. Contract `defs-v2026.09.08.4`, unchanged.
`shipping.config` unchanged. Predecessor `dsp4-s17-20260910.md` (dsp
`6235d22`).

**The default build does not move at all.** `DSP4_SHARED_KERNELS` defaults
to 0, and at 0 the image is `302d6142` / `3b3a6f8e` — byte for byte the
pair S17 left — from a tree regenerated by the new generator pass, with the
new `diag.h` field and the new build flag in place. That was checked before
anything was measured and again after the tree was regenerated in place.
What the session landed is behind that switch; what it found about chip 2
is behind no switch at all.

---

## 0. What S17 left, and what this session was asked to do

S17 closed every correctness item ahead of PW's window and filed one thing
it could not explain: **chip 2's D24 cost is boot-dependent by ten points**
on one image, one product and one measured clock — 92.88 % with zero
overruns on one boot and 102.83 % with 2.49 % of blocks missed on two
others, with `_proc_passes` tracking the shortfall exactly and chip 1 flat
to 0.3 % across the same boots. A capacity number that moves ten points
with nothing changed is not a number.

The other thing S17 left was the size of the remaining lever: chip 1's code
pool with 388 bytes of margin, of which 85 % is 32 copies of 9 per-strip
kernels.

---

## 1. Chip 2's ten points are SIGNAL, not boot

**Chip 2's ten points are its MAIN and SUB dynamics crossing threshold. The
cost is signal-dependent, not boot-dependent, and the D24 figure to quote
is the LOADED one — 112.3 % of budget with 10.9 % of blocks missed — not
the silent 92.8 %.**

### How it was found

`s18regime.sh` runs the capacity ladder N times and, on each boot and at
the same dwell, snapshots every chip-2 word that can put a per-block kernel
on a different branch (`tools/pi/dsp4_c2regime.py`, 347 words: the three
cascade classes' crossfade state, the dynamics engagement words and their
envelopes, the FX engines' bypass, the two live masks, and the one word
D71's CFG_COMMIT header lands in). Six boots of the S17 default build,
D24, clock measured on every row:

| boot | chip 1 | chip 2 | chip 2 overruns | chip-2 nodes with a live envelope (LIM / COMP / GATE) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| b1 | 71.69 % | 92.74 % | 0 | 5 / 0 / 0 |
| b2 | 71.65 % | 92.75 % | 0 | 5 / 0 / 0 |
| b3 | 71.84 % | 92.84 % | 0 | 5 / 0 / 0 |
| b4 | 71.69 % | **103.14 %** | **2.999 %** | **7 / 7 / 1** |
| b5 | 71.75 % | 92.67 % | 0 | 5 / 0 / 0 |
| b6 | 71.65 % | **95.84 %** | 0 | **6 / 5 / 0** |

Of 347 words read on every boot, **fifteen differ across boots and every
one of them is a dynamics envelope** — `_lim_envelope`, `_comp_envelope`
and one `_gate_envelope`, all on the MAIN / SUB / OCOMP / OLIM chain. Not
one crossfade word, not one engagement flag, not one mask, not the D71
word. On the expensive boot those envelopes are at or near the Q4.28
ceiling (`_comp_envelope_C2_MAIN_COMP` 0x7B3703CE, `_comp_envelope_C2_SUB_COMP`
0x7FFFFF4E); on the cheap boots they are zero and five limiters sit at
0x0000000C.

So the graph was carrying something on b4 and b6 and nothing on the other
four, and chip 2's dynamics have a cheap branch below threshold and an
expensive one above it. **The boot-to-boot variation was the bench being
silent on some boots and hot on others.**

### The controlled arm

The ladder above is an accident, so it was repeated deliberately.
`DSP4_PROFILE_SIGNAL=1` drives a square into every source; three boots,
D24:

| | chip 1 | chip 2 | chip 2 overruns |
|---|---|---|---|
| silent (b1–b3, b5) | 71.65–71.84 % | 92.67–92.84 % | 0 |
| 7 LIM + 7 COMP + 1 GATE engaged (b4) | 71.69 % | 103.14 % | 2.999 % |
| every source driven, ×3 boots | 112.99 / 113.04 / 113.03 % | **112.32 / 112.29 / 112.32 %** | **10.92 %** |

Reproducible to 0.03 points across three boots.

### What each number means, and the caveat that goes with it

**Chip 1's cost is signal-INDEPENDENT, and that is measured, not assumed.**
On b4 a near-full-scale signal reached chip 2's main bus, and it can only
have got there through chip 1's strips and mix fabric — yet chip 1 read
71.69 %, inside the spread of the four silent boots. Chip 1's COMP and GATE
call their gain computer on every sample whatever the level; there is no
cheap branch to take. **Chip 1's 113 % in the driven arm is therefore the
INSTRUMENT** — the square-wave synthesis `DSP4_PROFILE_SIGNAL` puts in its
46 input kernels — and is not a product figure.

**Chip 2's is a product figure, with one small caveat.** Chip 2's own
budget carries no mixing of its inputs, and its 92.7 → 112.3 % is the same
mechanism the boot ladder shows in the same direction and the same size.
The caveat: 37 of chip 2's RECV/XR nodes also carry the profile stimulus
(they have to — S12 found every IC RX slot reading zero under the default
config, so a chip-2 profile taken on the fabric alone measures LIMITER,
COMPRESSOR and GATE on exactly the cheap branch this section is about).
That synthesis is cheap and its cost is countable rather than estimated:
the stimulus loop is four instructions a sample where the production loop
is three — the production read is executed and discarded — so it is **one
instruction per sample per node, 37 x 16 = 592 cycles a block, 0.18 points**
of the 19.6. Netting it off leaves chip 2 at **~112.1 % of budget with its
dynamics engaged**, and the in-graph b4 point (103.1 % at 7 of 18 limiters,
7 of 10 compressors and 1 of 4 gates, with no stimulus at all) sits on the
same line. The stimulus is −6 dBFS by design, "above every default
threshold on chip 2", so the driven arm is the worst case it was built to
be.

### What follows

* **The capacity record is a SILENCE record.** Every `capacity.sh` figure
  in the tree was taken on a bench with no signal, and for chip 2 that is
  the cheap branch. The number a budget has to cover is the loaded one.
* **Chip 2 does not fit at D24 on the shipping default under load.**
  112.3 % of budget, 10.9 % of blocks missed, three boots.
* **`REPS=1` is not a capacity measurement** (S17's own conclusion) — but
  the reason is now known, and the fix is better than more repetitions:
  a capacity arm has to state which branch its dynamics were on.
* The lever is already on the table: **S16-6 put the LIMITER on
  `DSP4_DYN_LUT` at 253.3 → 92.1 c/sample-pair (−63.6 %)**, and chip 2 has
  18 limiters and 10 compressors. See §4.

## 2. One class made SHARED: the mechanism, and what the map says it is worth

**COMP is one body for 32 strips, behind `DSP4_SHARED_KERNELS=1`, and it
returns 28,800 bytes of chip 1's code pool — 12.3 % of the pool, against
the 388 bytes of margin S16 measured on the arm that carries the candidate
switches. TUBE went with it as a second measured point (§4) and returns
9,834 more; together 38,634.**

### The premise, checked rather than assumed

The nine 32-copy classes are not *nearly* identical. Normalise the node id
and the SIMD pool suffix and, for eight of the nine, all 32 emitted bodies
are the same text byte for byte. `gen_shared_kernels()` runs that
comparison on every generation and refuses to share a class whose copies
are more than one body — so the thing the change rests on is re-proved
every time the tree is generated, not recorded once here.

### Which class, and why not the biggest one

The map's own ranking, at the shipping default (`shared_kernel_survey.py`):

| class | copies | bytes each | total | % of pool | shareable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RTG | 32 | 1258 | 40,256 | 17.2 % | **no** — the 32 copies are 32 bodies |
| GATE | 32 | 1028 | 32,896 | 14.0 % | yes, spare i5/i6/i7 |
| **COMP** | 32 | 1000 | **32,000** | **13.6 %** | **yes — landed** |
| FILT | 32 | 968 | 30,976 | 13.2 % | yes, spare i5/i6/i7 |
| EQ | 32 | 664 | 21,248 | 9.1 % | yes, spare i6/i7 |
| DLY | 32 | 512 | 16,384 | 7.0 % | **no** — 9 distinct bodies |
| FDR | 32 | 444 | 14,208 | 6.1 % | yes, spare i3–i7 |
| **TUBE** | 32 | 390 | **12,480** | 5.3 % | **yes — landed as the second point** |
| GAIN | 32 | 198 | 6,336 | 2.7 % | **no** — 32 distinct bodies |

RTG is the biggest and it is not the one that was shared, for a stated
reason rather than a preference: its copies
differ in the STRIP INDEX as well as in the node id (`i1 = _xpc + 0` against
`_xpc + 16`, `dm(_rtg_busy + 0)` against `+ 16`), so a shared RTG needs a
second argument the other classes do not. Its body also touches all eight
DAG index registers, so the register the base would live in has to be
freed first. Both are solvable and neither is a session's afternoon; COMP
is the one that measures the ratio honestly, at 13.6 % of the pool.

### How a strip is addressed

A node's per-node `.var`s are declared consecutively in one object, so the
linker lays them out as a contiguous RECORD. Measured on the shipping map:
COMP's 32 records are 44 words apart to the word, GATE's 52, TUBE's 8,
GAIN's 12, FDR's 16, with no gap inside one. So the rewrite is

```
    r3 = dm(_comp_relq_C1_COMP_07);   ->   r3 = dm(SHK_COMP_OFF_comp_relq, i7);
```

a pre-modify with an immediate, which is **one instruction exactly as the
direct access was**. The kernel does not get slower per access; it pays an
ENTRY cost instead. The four DAG base loads and four address-to-register
loads become two instructions each, and the predecessor node's one-word
scalar buffer — which lives in another node's record — gets its own
pointer in i5. i6 is left alone deliberately: `dyn_lut_fx.asm` documents it
as the SIMD gain path's own.

The offsets are emitted as preprocessor EXPRESSIONS, not numbers, because
the record's shape is conditional — `DSP4_DYN_LUT` adds 342 words to the
middle of COMP's — and a number would be right for one build flag and
silently wrong for the other.

### The layout is not assumed

A shared kernel that addressed the wrong word would still link, would have
the same byte count and would look right in a listing, and would quietly
read strip 1's threshold from strip 7. So `tools/dsp/shared_kernel_check.py`
reads the linker map after every shared build and asserts, for every node
of every shared class, that each field is at base + the offset the kernel
uses; `build.sh` runs it and **a mismatch fails the build**. On the landed
image:

```
COMP     32 records, 43 words each, base stride [44]  OK
```

### It is a JUMP, not a call

The dispatch's arithmetic assumed the change ADDS a call per node per
block. It does not, and the reason matters for the whole decision: **the
graph has already paid a call per node per block since `DSP4_BLOCK_KERNELS`
landed** — `process_chain` does `call _C1_COMP_07_process` — so the stub
stands exactly where the body used to and the body's own `rts` returns to
the chain. On this part (D66, measured) a taken unconditional jump is
**+6.02 cycles** and a call/rts pair **+15.04**; the shared form pays the
cheaper one.

Both arms are in the tree at once, under `#if (DSP4_SHARED_KERNELS & 1)`,
so the switch is a build flag and not a regeneration, and the A/B below is
a paired measurement on one tree.

### Bytes, by the map

`dsp_codepool.py --diff`, chip 1, switch off → on:

| | bytes |
|---|---|
| each of 32 `C1_COMP_nn` objects | **−928** |
| `shared_kernels` | +896 |
| **TOTAL** | **−28,800** |

Boot stream 421,836 → 393,036 bytes. Chip 1's code section goes from
**235,064 of 262,144 bytes (89.7 %, 27,080 free) to 206,262 (78.7 %,
55,882 free)** — the change returns **more than the whole of the free space
it started with**.

A NUMBER FROM S16 THAT HAS TO BE READ WITH ITS ARM. S16 recorded chip 1's
margin as **388 bytes**, and this session's baseline reports 27,080 free.
Both are right and they are different arms: 388 bytes is the margin with
S16's candidate switches on (the dynamics table, the linear-threshold gate
and the block tap), which is the configuration the levers PW is weighing
would actually ship in; 27,080 is the shipping default with none of them.
Against the arm that matters, 28,800 bytes is **74 times the margin**; even
against the bare default it is more than everything free.

#### `shkstrip.sh` — the per-strip witness

The compressor's block-rate conversion reads `_comp_threshold_<nid>` and
writes `_comp_cgp_<nid>[0]`, both in that node's own record, once per
block. So: write a DIFFERENT threshold to each of several strips over SPI,
let a block run, and read each strip's own converted word back. A kernel on
one record leaves the other strips at their `.var` initialiser of zero.
It needs no signal and no capture.

**Run on both arms, and the control arm is what made it a bar.** Its first
run reported five of seven strips WRONG *on the switch-off control* — the
shipping image, `302d6142` — for two reasons that were both the witness's:
the model computed the conversion in float64 where the part does it in
float32 (1–3 LSB out on four strips), and it asked about strips 31 and 32
at D24, which the channel mask never calls at all, so their words are zero
however right the kernel is. Both fixed; the model is now the part's
arithmetic and the default strip list is inside the product.

Then, the two arms:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | −7.5 dB | 0xFD823098 | **0xFD823098** | 0xFD823098 |
| 2 | −9.0 | 0xFD02A0B4 | **0xFD02A0B4** | 0xFD02A0B4 |

## 4. The decision for PW: the other eight classes

### What a share costs, and why the cost barely depends on the class

The per-node STUB is the same for every class, because it is the same stub:
the block-pool prologue the body used to open with, the record base, the
predecessor pointer and a jump. **Measured at 72 bytes on both classes this
session landed** — COMP 1000 → 72 and TUBE 390 → 72 — so the bytes a class
returns are

```
    returned = n x each  -  n x 72  -  (one shared body)
```

and, with the shared body within a few tens of bytes of one inline copy,
that is `31 x each - 2,304`. Checked against the two landings: COMP
predicted 28,696 and measured **28,800**; TUBE predicted 9,786 and measured
**9,834**. Both classes together measure **−38,634**, which is their sum to
the byte — the shares are independent.

The CYCLES are also mostly fixed per node, and for the same reason: the
direct accesses cost what they cost either way (`dm(OFF, i7)` is one
instruction, as `dm(_absolute)` was), so what a class pays is the stub, the
jump, and one extra instruction for each base-or-address load it has to
rebuild from the record — times the block, for any of those that sit inside
the sample loop. COMP has one such load in its loop; TUBE has none.

| | bytes each | returns | c/node/block | points of D24 budget |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| COMP | 1000 | **28,800** | **~89** | **+0.64** at D24, +0.89 at D32 |
| TUBE | 390 | **9,834** | **~6** | **+0.04** — inside the boot-to-boot spread |

TUBE shared, on the part, three-boot mean against the off arm's:
`_proc_cyc` **71.71 % → 71.75 %**, worst block 71.88 % → 71.89 %, zero
overruns. Its whole cost is 131 cycles a block over 24 nodes — **about one
taken jump each (+6.02, D66)**, which is exactly what a class with no base
load and no address load to rebuild should pay.

**So the cost is not the stub and it is not the class's size: it is how
many RECORD-RELATIVE ACCESSES the kernel executes per block.** TUBE's
fourteen are all outside its sample loop and run once. COMP's run inside
it — five or six per sample, sixteen samples — plus the block-rate
conversion's forty-odd, plus one DAG base load per sample that has to be
rebuilt from the record base (`i0 = i7; modify(i0, OFF_comp_cgp)` where the
inline form had one immediate load). Two points, 6 and 89 cycles a node,
bracket the eight remaining classes rather than leaving them to an
estimate: a class is at TUBE's end if its record accesses are hoisted out
of the loop and at COMP's if they are not.

### The order to do them in, and what the pool would then hold

The order is bytes-first, because the cost is nearly flat per class and the
risk is not: every class on the "yes" list uses the same mechanism, the
same checked layout and the same per-strip witness.

1. **GATE** — 29,564 bytes, the largest remaining, and the class whose
   64 direct accesses make it the cheapest per byte (no base load in its
   loop at all).
2. **FILT** — 27,704. It has 34 base loads, so it is the one to measure
   rather than predict: base loads are the sites that cost an extra
   instruction each.
3. **EQ** — 18,280.
4. **FDR** — 11,460.

Done, with COMP and TUBE, that is **125,642 bytes — 53 % of chip 1's whole
code section**. Priced between the two measured points: GATE and EQ run
their record accesses inside a per-sample loop as COMP does and should be
read at COMP's end (~0.6 points each); FILT hoists its cascade state into
DAG registers before its loop and FDR is a per-block coefficient apply, so
both should be nearer TUBE's (~0.1). **Call it 1.5–2 points of D24 budget
for the four, and measure each as it lands rather than trusting the
bracket** — that is what the two points are for.

Then the two that need work before they are decisions at all:

* **RTG, 36,694 bytes and the single biggest class in the pool.** Two
  things have to change and both are ordinary: the strip index becomes a
  stub argument (it is already a literal in four places), and one DAG index
  register has to be freed in a body that currently uses all eight. Worth
  doing after the four above, because it is the largest single item left.
* **DLY, 13,568 bytes.** Its nine bodies differ in the delay pool slot,
  which is a per-node constant — the same shape of change as RTG's strip
  index, and smaller.
* **GAIN, 3,834 bytes.** Not worth the change on its own.

### What the pool would hold

Chip 1's code section is 262,144 bytes. At the shipping default it holds
235,064 with 27,080 free; S16 measured 388 bytes free on the arm carrying
the candidate switches. With COMP and TUBE shared it holds **196,366 with
65,778 free** — which is enough room for every lever currently queued and
the instruments beside them:

* `DSP4_DYN_LUT`'s 4,098 bytes of code (S15) and the LIMITER on the same
  machinery (S16-6) — the thing chip 2 needs, from §1.
* `DSP4_SCOPE_BLK_TAP`, the witness that S16 had to move objects around to
  fit at all.
* The 31-band GEQ on more outputs and the FX engines, which is what the
  pool is being cleared for.

### The recommendation, in one line

**Take GATE and FILT next (57 k bytes for well under a point), leave RTG
and DLY as scoped work rather than decisions, and — separately and more
urgently than any of this — put the LIMITER on the dynamics table, because
§1 says chip 2 does not fit at D24 under load and the code pool is no
longer what is stopping it.**

# S28 — the other products' fit, from the same silicon

Bench: rev C unit MW-D24-2, `app@192.168.1.219`. Contract `defs-v2026.09.08.4`,
unchanged — the submodule does not move in this session. Image under test is
the window candidate `shipping.config.s26`, and the build this session made
reproduces it **byte for byte**: chip1 `6396187c`, chip2 `9222c2ee`, which is
what `~/dspboot/s26_*` has held since S27. No deploy.

Seven sessions priced D24 and D32 driven to the tenth of a point. The range
has more products than two, and the scoreboard's "does it fit" table had two
rows. It now has four, every one of them generated from the product's own
definition and every one of them measured on the part — the two new products
predicted first and measured after, so the prediction could be scored.

---

## 0. What this session settled

1. **D16 and D12 are the same silicon, the same firmware and the same
   address map — they are two config words.** Their cell sets are STRICT
   SUBSETS of D24's and of D32's: `cells(D12) ⊂ cells(D16) ⊂ cells(D24)`,
   0 cells outside, and every one of D16's 2,571 addressed cells and D12's
   1,777 lands on the SAME chip, page and address the D32 map already gives
   it. Not one new address exists in the range's two smallest products.
2. **Both fit with room, driven, with the load, on the part.** D16 reads
   **41.69 % of chip 1 and 73.02 % of chip 2**; D12 reads **33.26 % and
   66.59 %**; **zero missed blocks** on either product, on either chip, over
   two boots each. The closest to the edge in the range is still D32 chip 2
   at **100.82 % with 1,943 blocks of 270,000 missed** — S24's finding and
   S27's, unchanged by anything here and reproduced to the block.
3. **D32 Rack, D32C and D32R have no fit row because they have no
   definition.** `defs/products/d32{rack,c,r}/` carry an intake report and
   nothing else, whose first line is *"no generated master cell list — run
   the def pipeline first"*. There is no product def and no mx-master, so
   there is no cell count to generate. That is a hub item, named rather
   than guessed at.
4. **The construction under-predicts, in the safe direction, and by how
   much is now measured.** A two-point interpolation of D24 and D32 lands
   chip 1 within **+0.74…+2.45** points and chip 2 within **+1.80…+5.35** on
   products a half and a third their size, and **every one of the sixteen
   deltas has the same sign**. Both anchors were re-measured the same night
   and reproduce S27 to **−0.18…+0.27 points over sixteen comparisons**, so
   the residual is the product and not the instrument. The mechanism is in
   §2.3 and it is the **D32 scope class** riding on the D24→D32 line.
5. **A live FX engine costs 3.26–3.39 points of chip 2 and nothing at all
   of chip 1**, flat across four products and two engine counts (D12 3.31,
   D16 3.26, D24 3.39, D32 3.37; chip 1 −0.09…+0.15). The product def names
   the engine count, so this belongs in the construction — and it was not in
   the first cut of it (S28-3).
6. **The FX regime bar could not pass on a product with fewer than six FX
   engines** (S28-2), and D16's first driven row is where it showed: the
   check enumerated all six engines off the SYMBOL TABLE while `loadfx`
   writes the families the LANDED MAP names, which for a D16 is four. Fixed
   the way the dynamics half of the same tool was already right — the
   product's count, with the superset's extras reported and not required.
7. **`CFG_PRODUCT_ID` is a scope class, not a product identity** (S28-4).
   It selects one of two `scope=` runs in the graph, and there is no third
   value; D16 and D12 boot as scope class 1 and the part cannot tell them
   from a D24 by that word. It tells them apart by `_chan_mask_live` /
   `_aux_mask_live`, which is what the rows below are identified by.
8. **The CFG2 widening is designed and not applied** (§3). There are **no
   free bits in `DIAG_BUILD_CFG2` at all** — the comment that said
   otherwise was naming the zero bits of its own signature (S28-5) — so the
   fix is a third word. `shipping.config.s21` would read `0xC30003FA` and
   `shipping.config.s26` `0xC3000FFA`; `cfg_words.py --design-cfg3` prints
   them today and nothing in the firmware produces them.

## 1. Gate 1 — by construction

### 1.1 Where a product's numbers come from

Three inputs, each from the one place that owns it, and nothing counted
twice:

| | source |
|---|---|
| cell count | `defs/gen/matrix/<p>-mx-master.csv`, expanded by `defs/tools/expand_matrix.py` into `MW/<P>/MX/_matrix.csv` (`./sync-defs.sh`) |
| addressed cells | `MW/D32/DSP/gen_dsp.py --propose`: the superset graph intersected with that product's cell set → `proposals/defs/products/<p>/dsp.csv` |
| node census | `MW/D32/DSP/SHARC/dsp.csv` (698 nodes) resolved against the product's three config words, with the SAME rules the generator emits the gates with |
| capacity | a two-point interpolation of S27's measured DRIVEN rows on D24 and D32 |

`tools/dsp/product_fit.py` is the whole of it, and it reads
`dsp_codegen.STRIP_NODE_RE`, `STRIP_MTR_RE` and `aux_owner` directly rather
than restating which node belongs to which strip.

**D12 and D16 joined the contract flow this session.** `sync-defs.sh` now
expands four products, `validate-matrix-contract.py` checks all four for
MxAdd continuity and family containment, `check-contract-drift.sh` watches
both new matrices, and `defs.lock` gained four declaration hashes and two
matrix hashes. The change is purely ADDITIVE: `D24_*` and `D32_*` are
byte-identical in the new lock and the contract version does not move.

**D16 and D12 are `PROPOSAL_ONLY_PRODUCTS` in `gen_dsp.py`, deliberately.**
The contract products are still d32 and d24 — they are the ones whose landed
`dsp.csv` every generated firmware artifact is derived from, and whose
absence is fatal. Nothing in `defs/` has landed a d16 or d12 address map, so
adding them to `check_proposal()` would be asserting a contract that does not
exist. `--propose` writes them; nothing consumes them until the hub lands
them.

### 1.4 What the construction cannot see, and it is the interesting part

**The firmware has exactly three product words, and only two of them are
counts.** `CFG_CHAN_MASK` gates channel strips, `CFG_AUX_MASK` gates aux
chains, `CFG_PRODUCT_ID` selects one of two scope classes. Nothing else in
the product definition reaches the firmware at all:

| | D12 | D16 | D24 | D32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| groups | **2 of 4** | 4 of 4 | 4 of 4 | 4 of 4 |
| FX engines | **4 of 6** | **4 of 6** | 6 of 6 | 6 of 6 |
| matrix buses | **0 of 4** | **0 of 4** | **2 of 4** | 4 of 4 |
| DCAs | **4 of 8** | **4 of 8** | 8 of 8 | 8 of 8 |

Every bold entry is a node the one firmware calls on a product that does not
define it. They are on their cheap branch — nothing addressed them, nothing
fed them — but "called on the cheap branch" and "not called" are not the same
thing, and this project has the number for the difference: when
`CFG_CHAN_MASK` and `CFG_AUX_MASK` got readers on 2026-09-09, skipping eight
strips and four aux chains a D24 does not have was worth **18.08 % of chip 1's
budget and 10.81 % of chip 2's**.

So a D12 that wanted its two unused groups, its four matrix buses and its two
surplus FX engines skipped would need a word that says so, and there is no
such word. That is not a problem for the fit — §2 measures D16 clearing its
tighter chip by 27 points and D12 by 33 — but it is the honest reading of what
"by construction" means here, and it is the same shape as the CFG2 widening
of §3: a configuration the part cannot be told about.

## 2. Gate 2 — driven, on the part

### 2.1 The arm

One image, four products, two boots each. `capacity.sh --driven` with
`SETUP_MODE=loadfx FXTYPE=3 FXTYPES="off"`, `REPS=2`,
`SHIPPING_CONFIG=shipping.config.s26`,
`DSP_LANDED_DIR=proposals/defs/products`, on the `driveall` LOGIC bitstream
with the CM4 playing. The CPLD was re-flashed at the top of the session and
again to restore it: IDCODE `0x020a30dd` before and after both times,
**FLASH OK on attempt 1** both times, 40,790 lines of SVF, chain reached its
shutdown — S27-5's check, in force.

**The build is the candidate.** `chip1.ldr 6396187c`, `chip2.ldr 9222c2ee`,

### The mechanism, visible only with four points

On row A — silent, the configuration the product boots with, the one row no
parameter write has touched — the per-unit slopes between ADJACENT products
are:

| segment | chip 1, pts/strip | chip 2, pts/aux bus |
|---|--:|--:|
| D12 → D16 | +1.77 | +2.32 |
| D16 → D24 | +1.74 | +2.39 |
| **D24 → D32** | **+1.82** | **+3.29** |

The first two segments agree to 0.03 and 0.07. The third is dearer on both
chips, and the reason is not strips and not aux buses: **D32 is the only
product in the range that boots scope class 0**, and scope class 0 runs 32
nodes the others gate off — eight snake returns (`C1_XIN_SNK_*`), their eight
chip-1 transfers, their eight chip-2 receives and the eight chip-2 aux inputs.
The D24→D32 anchor line carries that step as though it were per-strip and
per-aux cost, and extrapolating it down to twelve channels subtracts it four
times over.

That is the whole of the residual, and it is a lesson about the method rather
than about the products: **two anchors that differ in three things cannot
separate those three things.** This pair differs in channel count, aux count
AND scope class. The fit table now has four anchors, and `product_fit.py`
prints the per-segment slopes so the step is visible instead of averaged into
a line.

The loaded rows (B, D, C) are noisier on chip 2's aux axis — D16 → D24 reads
+5.69 on row B and +2.15 on row D — because `--mode load` opens the families
THAT PRODUCT'S CELLS name, and the products differ by more than their aux
count: D24 has two matrix buses and D32 four, D16 and D12 have none. Row A is
the clean size axis and that is why the slope table is quoted from it.

### Where a product's graph would NOT build

Nowhere, and the reason is worth stating plainly rather than leaving as an
absence. **There is nothing to build.** One firmware serves the range
(decision D3), the image is identical byte for byte on all four rows above,
and a product is three words the host writes at CONFIG_COMMIT. No product in
the table needs a lane or a converter the rev C card lacks, because no product
in the table is a superset of D32 — every one is a subset.

What a D16 or a D12 DOES leave is idle hardware on this bench. The rev C unit
is a D24 board, and the `driveall` bitstream broadcasts the CM4's playback
onto every DSPA input lane; the mask decides which of those strips RUN, so
sixteen (D16) or twelve (D12) of the twenty-four are inside it and the rest
are skipped. The masks
are read back LIVE off both parts before any row is taken
(`_chan_mask_live` / `_aux_mask_live`), so a row can never be mislabelled as a
product it was not configured as.

## 3. Gate 3 — the CFG2 widening, designed

### 3.1 The hole, restated with the bit map

S27-3 found it in the window candidate's own `cfgverify` output:
`DIAG_BUILD_CFG2` carries **two bits of a four-bit `DSP4_SHARED_KERNELS`
mask** — bit 5 is mask bit 0 (COMP), bit 15 is mask bit 1 (TUBE), and S26's
GATE and FILT have no bit at all. So `shipping.config.s21` (mask 3) and
`shipping.config.s26` (mask 15) both read back

    DIAG_BUILD_CFG  0xCF45FF10     DIAG_BUILD_CFG2  0xC2019E6F

and `cfgverify`'s "the part matches to the bit" is true of both. The window
candidate and its rollback pair are indistinguishable by the words that exist
so a mismeasured configuration is never silent.

**And there are no spare bits in that word (S28-5).** `cfg_words.py` said
*"free bits in w2 for the fix: 24 and 26-29"* and those are the ZERO BITS OF
THE 0xC2 SIGNATURE, not spare field space — bit 24 in particular is exactly
what distinguishes `0xC2` from a `0xC3`. Enumerating the layout rather than
eyeballing it: **every one of bits 0..31 has an owner.**

| bits | field |
|---|---|
| 31..24 | `0xC2` signature |
| 23..16 | `DSP4_BLOCK_DECIMATE` |
| 15 | `DSP4_SHARED_KERNELS` bit 1 |
| 14..13 | `DSP4_BQ_SIMD_PIPE` |
| 12 | `DSP4_C2_BQ_GRAPH` |
| 11 | `DSP4_GATE_LINTHR` |
| 10 | `DSP4_DYN_LUT` |
| 9..8 | `DSP4_TX_EARLY` |
| 7 | `DSP4_FX_TYPE_DECLARED` |
| 6 | `DSP4_GATHER_FIRST` |
| 5 | `DSP4_SHARED_KERNELS` bit 0 |
| 4 | `DSP4_SCOPE_BLK_TAP != 0` |
| 3 | `DSP4_SIMD_STRIPS` |
| 2 | `DSP4_SIMD_GRAPH` |
| 1 | `DSP4_SIMD_DYN` |
| 0 | `DSP4_STRIP_FUSED` |

So the fix is a THIRD word, for exactly the reason `DIAG_BUILD_CFG2` itself
exists: the first word ran out (S10-5, S11-1) and the answer was another word,
not a re-layout.

### 3.2 The design

**`DIAG_BUILD_CFG3` at `0xE0EC`**, signature `0xC3`:

| bits | field | why |
|---|---|---|
| 31..24 | `0xC3` | distinct from `0xCF` and `0xC2` in bits 27..24 |
| 23 | **INSTRUMENT** | 1 if ANY switch in the instrument set is off its shipping default. A shipping image reads 0, always. |
| 22..16 | `DSP4_STRIPS` | the COMPILE-TIME strip cut, 0..127 |
| 15..8 | `DSP4_SHARED_KERNELS` | the WHOLE mask, eight classes' worth |
| 7 | `DSP4_RTG_FABRIC` | |
| 6 | `DSP4_GAIN_SIMD` | |
| 5 | `DSP4_DLY_SPLIT` | |
| 4 | `DSP4_C2_XPAIR` | |
| 3..2 | `DSP4_DYN_INLINE` | |
| 1 | `DSP4_SPI_PARTIAL_FIX2` | |
| 0 | `DSP4_DYN_TABLES` | |

Three things about that layout are decisions and not defaults.

**`DIAG_BUILD_CFG2` IS NOT RE-LAID OUT.** Its two legacy `SHARED_KERNELS`
bits stay exactly where they are, so **its value does not change for any
image** and every decoder that reads it — `cfg_words.py`, `dsp4_buildcfg.py`,
`check_shipping_config.sh`, the bench — keeps working unchanged. `CFG3` is
what tells mask 3 from mask 15. A re-layout would have invalidated every
`0xC2...` word in the record for no gain.

**The mask gets EIGHT bits, not four.** Four is what the mask needs today and
four is what ran out last time. GATE and FILT were the third and fourth
classes; `shared_kernel_survey.py` is the tool that finds the fifth.

**Bit 23 is the one that pays for the word.** `DIAG_BUILD_CFG2` was born
because three arms 81,299 cycles/block apart read back the same first word
(S11-1), and the capacity record spent a fortnight quoting an instrument for
the product. Twenty switches turn an image into an instrument —
`DSP4_PROFILE_SIGNAL`, `DSP4_BLOCK_DECIMATE != 1`, `DSP4_NODE_LIMIT`,
`DSP4_STRIPS`, `DSP4_TXPROBE`, `DSP4_BISECT`, the probes, the self-tests, the
shootouts — and none of them can be seen from a single word today. One bit
makes *"is this the product or an instrument?"* a question the PART answers.
The set is `cfg_words.INSTRUMENT_SWITCHES` and it is a list, not a heuristic.

Sixty-one of build.sh's eighty-six `DSP4_*` switches are carried by NO
config word at all. `CFG3` takes the nine that change a shipping image and
flags the rest through bit 23; the remainder are negative controls whose
value only matters inside the session that set them.

### 3.3 The numbers, printable today

`tools/dsp/cfg_words.py --design-cfg3` computes the proposed word from the
same three sources the existing tool uses — the config file, `build.sh`'s
defaults and the derivations — and is deliberately **not** called by
`words()` and **not** compared by `--check`: no image produces this word yet,
and a host that expected one would fail every bar in the release window.

| configuration | CFG | CFG2 | **CFG3 would be** |
|---|---|---|---|
| `shipping.config` | `0xCF45FF10` | `0xC2010244` | `0xC30000FA` |
| `shipping.config.s21` | `0xCF45FF10` | `0xC2019E6F` | **`0xC30003FA`** |
| `shipping.config.s26` | `0xCF45FF10` | `0xC2019E6F` | **`0xC3000FFA`** |

**The two words that are identical today differ in the third.** And the
instrument bit works both ways: `DSP4_PROFILE_SIGNAL=1` over s26 gives
`0xC3800FFA`, and so does `DSP4_BLOCK_DECIMATE=32` — two different
instruments, both of them no longer able to look like the product.

### 3.4 The md5 consequence — every quoted image moves

The word has to EXIST in the image to be read out of it: one `.var` in
`diag.asm`'s DM block and one compare in `_diag_rd`'s address ladder. Both
chips' `.ldr` change. That is the whole cost of this change and it is why it
waits for the window rather than landing inside it.

Every image md5 the record currently quotes moves on the first rebuild after
the change:

### 3.5 The apply step, for after PW signs

Four edits, and they are named here so the next session does not have to
re-derive them. Deliberately not scripted: a script that applies this would
be this change, and the gate says designed, not applied.

1. `MW/D32/DSP/SHARC/src/diag.h` — `#define DIAG_BUILD_CFG3 0xE0EC` and
   `DIAG_BUILD_CFG3_VALUE`, laid out as §3.2, with the `INSTRUMENT` term.
2. `MW/D32/DSP/SHARC/src/diag.asm` — `.var _diag_build_cfg3 =
   DIAG_BUILD_CFG3_VALUE;` beside `_diag_build_cfg2`, and one compare in
   `_diag_rd` beside the `DIAG_BUILD_CFG2` one.
3. `tools/dsp/cfg_words.py` — `words()` returns three, `--check` takes three,
   and `unrepresented()` loses its `DSP4_SHARED_KERNELS` entry because the
   word now carries it. `design_cfg3()` becomes the implementation.
4. `tools/pi/dsp4_buildcfg.py` — `DIAG_BUILD_CFG3 = 0xE0EC`, `decode3()`,
   `describe3()`; `cfgverify.sh` passes all three words; the caveat line that
   S27 added beside the pass comes out.

Then: rebuild `shipping.config.s21` and `shipping.config.s26`, confirm the
part reads `0xC30003FA` and `0xC3000FFA`, re-stage both pairs under new
names, and re-record the md5 table in `window-candidate.md`. The audio bars
(`busgold`, `goldnode`, `famverify`) must reproduce their stored values
exactly — if any moves, the change touched something it had no business
touching.

## 4. Findings

Full text in `findings.md`.

| | |
|---|---|
| **S28-1** | D16 and D12 are not new products to the DSP; they are two config words, and their address maps cost nothing — strict cell subsets of D24 and D32, 0 disagreements over 2,571 and 1,777 addressed cells. The three D32 variants have no fit row because they have no definition. |
| **S28-2** | the FX regime bar could not pass on a product with fewer than six FX engines — a bar whose SCOPE is the superset and whose STIMULUS is the product. Fixed the way the dynamics half of the same tool was already right. |
| **S28-3** | the construction had no FX-engine term, and an FX engine is 3.26–3.39 points of chip 2 (four products, two engine counts) and nothing at all of chip 1. |
| **S28-4** | `CFG_PRODUCT_ID` is a scope class, not a product identity, and there is no third value. D16 and D12 boot as a D24 and are told apart by the mask words. |
| **S28-5** | `DIAG_BUILD_CFG2` has no free bits at all, and the comment that named some was naming its own signature. |
| **S28-6** | the two-anchor construction under-predicts every smaller product by +0.74…+5.35 points; both controls reproduce to ±0.27, so it is the product and not the night, and the mechanism is the D32 SCOPE CLASS riding on the D24→D32 line. |

## 1. Why f6c146c took MW-D24-2 down (records)

The firmware was not the cause. On 09-15 the sequencing app (f6c146c, `a4e15bbb`) was flashed with the old `flash-app.sh` (`reset halt` → `program` → `reset run`). The chain of events (mx26 `tasks.md` INCIDENT 10:58 09-15) was:

1. `reset halt` releases U34's enable pins.
2. The 09-13 pull-downs then switch the digital bucks off.
3. +5V5 was at ~4.5 V (interim R20 100K), below the CM4's brown-out.
4. The UPS controller latched in backup mode on the part-charged supercap, and the CM4 stayed dark.

Recovery needed 12 V off and a supercap discharge. The read-back afterwards showed the OLD app intact (len 0x6A0) and the new image never written. Three fixes followed:

- **Flow:** hot-attach only (baf681b, PW 09-18).
- **Hardware:** rocker-follow pull-ups from 3V3_SW (rev C hand mod, rev D copper).
- **Power stage:** R20 → 115K.

The f6c146c code (PSU_DIG_EN 30 ms after UPS_EN from standby, duty ramps) is therefore reused unchanged in the app. The new parts are in the bootloader (power-on stagger) and around it.

Records wrinkle (hub item): mx26 `d24-firmware.csv` declares PWR = `a4e15bbb` (f6c146c), an image that was never flashed. The unit runs 0215bc4 (len 0x6A0, rebuild `b3ca023a`) behind the 08-18 bootloader (`43bedeb0`).

## 2. What changed

**Bootloader (`boot.c`, `bootcmd.c`), rails first.** The first code after reset reads PF0 and drives UPS_EN/PSU_DIG_EN to match:

- **Rocker OFF:** both low.
- **Rocker ON after a warm reset** (SYSRESETREQ, NRST): both high.
- **Rocker ON after power-on/brown-out** (`RCC_CSR.PORRSTF`): UPS_EN high, PSU_DIG_EN low for 30 ms, then high. This is the stagger.

Writes go through BSRR on PA2/PA3 only.

Then the mailbox is checked (top 8 bytes of SRAM; both images now start SP at 0x20000FF8). Then the app header and CRC are checked: jump, or enter update mode.

**Update mode** is option 2: the host link on AN_EN/PI_SD with ERASE (keyed), WRITE (64 B, resend-safe), READ, CHECK, GO.

- **LED:** the triple-blink safe loop is kept for "no valid app". A double blink means the app asked for update mode.
- **Rails:** they keep following the rocker (debounced, staggered), which is what the hardware does with the part in reset. There is no orderly save here, so do not switch off mid-update.
- **Timeout:** a requested session idle for 120 s returns to a valid app.
- **Memory:** no `.data`/`.bss` (the linker asserts it), so the app's reset log and the mailbox survive.

**App (`main.c`, `p1.c`).**

1. f6c146c stagger and duty ramps unchanged: 12CLK 2→8 over 100 ms, then 48CLK 8→32 over 100 ms.
2. The host link is polled between ticks against TIM17 (1 µs).
3. AN_EN's analog meaning is now its level held for 20 ms (`CFG_ANEN_STABLE_MS`), taken from the link's pulse-free idle level. Frames never move the rails. ST_BOOT→ST_ON and the ST_ON clock control use it. **This adds 20 ms to AN_EN response.**
4. Commands: PING (state, rails, clocks, pedal telemetry including the last probe's ADC codes, reset count), P1_ALIVE, P1_HOLD (1..60 s, re-armable), P1_RELEASE, P1_CYCLE (100..10 000 ms), and ENTER_BOOT (keyed, refused while the converter clocks run, so AN_EN goes low first; mailbox + SYSRESETREQ).
5. J8 pedal supply (rev D CHANGE 6), worked through in §4.

**Link (`link.c`, `proto.h`).** Pulse-width code: 0 = 200 µs, 1 = 600 µs, START = 1 400 µs, gaps of 200 µs, and 3 ms of quiet ends a frame. A pulse is an excursion away from the line's idle level, so it works with AN_EN high or low. Frames carry CRC-16. About 0.6 ms per bit, so a 3.9 KB app takes roughly 25 s and a full 12 KB roughly 75 s.

PI_SD idles low with pulses under 2 ms. The app's gpiomon watch (200 ms debounce) cannot see them. It also holds GPIO4, so the host tool needs matrix-app stopped.

## 3. Soft start and the rev D AC-coupled gate drive

The ramp is a duty ramp at a fixed 1 MHz. Each gate pulse lasts 50 ns at compare 2 and rises to 200 ns at 8 (±15 V) and to 800 ns at 32 (phantom). How it interacts with the rev D drive (series C → gate, 4K99 gate to GND, PMEG4010 DC restore):

1. **The DC-restore diode is what makes the phantom ramp work.** An AC-coupled gate without the clamp swings V·(1−D), and that shrinks as the duty rises. The phantom gate amplitude would fall from ~9.6 V at 20 % to ~2.4 V at 80 % during the 48CLK ramp, so the FET would drop out of enhancement mid-ramp. With the clamp, the swing stays at the full drive at every duty. The rev D drawing has the clamp on all five, and it must not be depopulated.
2. **The duty changes slowly** (one compare step every 16 ms on 12CLK, every ~4 ms on 48CLK), against a coupling time constant of ~50 µs (4K99 × 10 nF). The coupling cap tracks every step. No transient, nothing to change.
3. **Shortest pulse.** 50 ns at the MCU pin passes the AND gate (~5 ns) and the MCP14A0151 (tens of ns delay plus rise/fall). The FET may see a very short or partial pulse for the first step. That is the intent ("below 2 the FET barely switches"), and the AC coupling does not shorten it further: the series R × Ciss is a few ns. Bench: scope the gate at compare 2.
4. **Stop and reset.** CCR = 0 parks the pin low. A hi-Z pin (any reset) also stops the clock, and through the AC coupling the gate decays off in ~50 µs. The coupling makes every stop path safe.
5. **Open item from the records.** The rev B errata describe the clock as "AC-coupled, pull-down after the cap" at the AND input. If that RC is long compared with 1 µs, the AND sees the same duty-dependent shift, and 80 % could fall below the gate's V_IH. It evidently works today (the phantom runs), but the 5 % and 80 % ends of the ramp are the cases to scope at the AND input.

## 4. P1 pedal supply (rev D CHANGE 6), firmware side

The pins:

- **PA6 P1_EN** is active low. It is driven high (clamped) from `gpio_init` onward, and hi-Z at reset is also clamped (4K7 base pull-up).
- **PA7 P1_SENSE** idles as an input with the internal pull-down. It is switched to analog only for the ~17 µs conversion.
- **On rev C** (PA7 N/C) the pull-down holds the pin at 0 V, so every probe reads 0/0, which means "no signature", and PA6 is never driven low. The p1 test confirms 21 probes in 60 s with 9 V never on.

The probe sequence, once the rails are up:

1. Wait 500 ms.
2. Drive PA7 low for 500 ms (the "re-probe" discharge through 22K).
3. Release it and read the ADC at 30 ms and at 400 ms.

A pedal's ~10 µF input caps crawl to the final level with τ ≈ 0.23 s (100K ∥ 30K, desk arithmetic): about 12 % at 30 ms and 82 % at 400 ms. An open jack is already at its level at 30 ms. A short or an Ethernet port never rises. 9 V goes on only on that signature. While clamped, it re-probes every 2 s.

**Thresholds are [TBD bench]** (`config.h`). The model assumes a final code of ~250. The diode-set floors move with temperature, and the internal pull-down's load is in the reading.

Heartbeat and hold:

- **Heartbeat.** U34 cannot hear the pedal; its UART goes to H1S4. The heartbeat is therefore the host's P1_ALIVE, which the D24 app sends while it hears the pedal. The first beat is owed within 10 s of 9 V on, then at least every 3 s; otherwise the supply re-clamps and re-probes.
  🔴 **Until the D24 app sends P1_ALIVE, a rev D pedal is powered ~10 s out of every ~11.4 s.** That is CHANGE 6 as drawn. `CFG_P1_HEARTBEAT_REQUIRED 0` disables the re-clamp if PW wants pedals live on rev D before the app work.
- **UPDATE HOLD.** P1_HOLD holds the supply and suspends the heartbeat check for up to 60 s. Each re-arm restarts the 60 s. Expiry gives one heartbeat window, then the normal check resumes.
- **Power-cycle.** P1_CYCLE clamps at once, waits off_ms, then re-probes (the 500 ms discharge plus 400 ms measure), so 9 V never goes back on without the signature. 1 000 ms off gives the supply back after 1.9 s in the model. The host opens the H1S4 relay at 8E1 first, as S167/S169 do, so the P1 ROM auto-bauds on the host's first byte.
- **Rails and resets.** Rails down (STANDBY/SHUTDOWN) clamps and drops the hold. ENTER_BOOT clamps before the reset.

## 5. Option 4 (ROM bootloader on a blank U34): blocked by hardware

`tools/pi/d24_pwrrom.py` is written: AN3155 through `d24_pedal.Rom`, a raw 8E1 tty, F030x4 PID 0x444, legacy or extended erase, boot @0x08000000 and app @0x08001000 each read back, then WRP sector 0 or Go. It passes against the ROM model. It cannot reach a U34 as the nets are known:

1. **The STM32F030x4 has no empty-flash check.** RM0360 says only the F070x6 and F030xC have one ([ST community: empty check mechanism](https://community.st.com/s/article/Empty-check-mechanism-on-STM32)). A blank part with BOOT0 low starts from empty flash, not the ROM. U34 BOOT0 = BUS_BOOT with R103 10K to GND, and NRST goes only to J32 (mx26 `docs/d24-signals.csv` G3438/G3440). The CM4 can drive neither.
2. **PA9/PA10 are not a CM4 UART.**
   - PA10 = AN_EN = CM4 GPIO26, which is not a UART TX. A bit-banged TX is possible.
   - PA9 = PWR_FAIL = CM4 WL_nDisable/BT_nDisable, which the CM4 cannot read. The ROM ACKs every step, so receive is essential.
   - The dispatch says the rev D U34 nets are identical except PA6/PA7. R137 adds pulls; it does not add routing.

I did not see the rev D netlist (it is on PETERBC/SharePoint). If rev D already routes BOOT0/NRST and a UART pair, the tool runs as is, with `--port`.

🔴 **PW decision:** (a) drop option 4. A blank U34 is programmed over SWD (the 10-14 ruling: rocker-follow pull-ups power the CM4, mux ch1 / fifth channel), and option 2 updates any part that has this bootloader. Or (b) a rev D copper change: BOOT0 and NRST from CM4 GPIOs, and PA9/PA10 to a CM4 UART pair (which then needs PWR_FAIL and AN_EN moved). **Recommendation: (a).** SWD already covers the blank part, and option 2 covers every later update without SWD, including an interrupted SWD flash.
